Genome-wide comparison of predicted splicing effects between OpenSpliceAI and SpliceAI

Abstract

OpenSpliceAI reproduces the SpliceAI architecture in PyTorch and supports retraining with different annotations and species. Here, we compare their predicted variant effects across the published collection of masked genic single-nucleotide variants. The frozen analysis contains 3,334,708,099 matched variant–gene annotations. Agreement differs by event: acceptor and donor loss scores have Pearson correlations of 0.900 and 0.906, compared with 0.681 and 0.597 for gains. At a cutoff of 0.5, OpenSpliceAI makes more loss calls and fewer gain calls, with the largest reduction in donor gain. Among jointly called gains, the models usually select the same position. On identical observations shared by two OpenSpliceAI training seeds and SpliceAI, gain-score differences exceed the observed seed difference more strongly than loss-score differences. These results describe agreement between a published ensemble and individual retrained checkpoints; they do not establish relative biological accuracy. We report inference progress separately from the frozen scientific results, which remain provisional because scoring is incomplete.

Introduction

OpenSpliceAI implements the SpliceAI architecture in PyTorch and supports retraining with different annotations and species [2]. Similar splice-site prediction performance does not establish that two trained models assign the same effect to a sequence variant. Here, we compare OpenSpliceAI and SpliceAI across the published collection of masked genic single-nucleotide variants (SNVs), asking where their predicted effects agree and which types of effects differ most.

We distinguish four events: acceptor gain (AG), acceptor loss (AL), donor gain (DG), and donor loss (DL). Gains summarize increases in predicted splice-site scores after substitution; losses summarize decreases. Each event score records the largest change within the search window and has an associated selected position. A variant can receive multiple nonzero event scores. These events are therefore not mutually exclusive variant classes. MAX, the largest of the four scores, summarizes predicted effect strength without distinguishing gain from loss or acceptor from donor.

Our comparisons address score magnitude, overlap of calls at a common cutoff, boundary proximity, and selected positions. We also compare two OpenSpliceAI training seeds on identical observations. SpliceAI is the comparator throughout. The collection contains no experimental splicing outcomes, so concordance identifies similarities and differences between predictions without determining which model is correct when they disagree.
