Supplement: genome-wide OpenSpliceAI and SpliceAI comparison

Abstract

We provide detailed methods and supporting analyses for the genome-wide comparison of OpenSpliceAI and SpliceAI. The supplement documents counting units, pairing, masking, score precision, annotation context, and matched seed comparisons. It records the review of all sixteen original figures and preserves complete data access alongside the concise six-figure main report.

This supplement accompanies the main report. The scientific results remain the September 9 snapshot. The separately dated progress update does not add newly scored chunks to any scientific comparison. Figures S1–S11 retain the supporting evidence while the main report focuses on four predicted events: acceptor gain (AG), acceptor loss (AL), donor gain (DG), and donor loss (DL). MAX is the largest of these scores.

Detailed methods

Scoring collection and models

We used the published masked genic SNV collection distributed with SpliceAI. The collection includes genic positions and flanking sequence and does not enumerate all possible substitutions at every reference base. The campaign divides the source into 100,000 chunks. We scored MANE-trained OpenSpliceAI checkpoints from seeds rs10 and rs13, each using a single checkpoint. The published SpliceAI scores were produced by a five-model ensemble. The seed labels do not refer to cross-validation folds. The comparison therefore combines differences in training, checkpoints, implementation and ensembling.

The recorded setup uses GRCh38.p14, the GENCODE v24 canonical gene table supplied with SpliceAI, a 10,000-nt model context, a ±50-bp event-search window, and masked output. Training annotations and scoring annotations have different roles: MANE defines the retraining data, whereas the campaign's shared canonical gene table controls assignment and masking. For each event, the score is the largest predicted increase or decrease in the event-search window; its accompanying delta position records the selected location relative to the variant.

Audit, pairing and comparison domains

Input and output VCFs were audited for completeness, parseability, source-record preservation and annotation structure. The analysis pins source artifacts and manifests by checksum and verifies selected chunk identities. A valid structural audit does not establish the checkpoint that produced a legacy file. We retain the classification legacy_unprovenanced and do not infer embedded provenance receipts from campaign settings alone.

We pair by chromosome, position, reference allele, alternate allele and exact gene label. We collapse identical duplicate values and exclude conflicting duplicates. An absent model score is missing, not zero. Chunk-boundary joining resolves groups split by file partitioning. We retain separate counts for physical VCF records, distinct variant alleles, gene annotations and paired observations.

Streaming summaries and numerical verification

The analysis reduces streams of paired annotations to additive counts, moments, fixed-bin histograms, and stratum tables. It does not materialize a multi-billion-row dataframe. Exact threshold counters use 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5 and 0.8, with positive calls defined by score ≥ cutoff. The 200-bin score histograms have width 0.005; conditional quantiles and threshold sweeps derived from them are approximate. Empty bins remain gaps. Undefined rates remain undefined rather than being replaced with zero.

All event-level global score summaries use the full paired domain, including shared zeros. Pearson correlation describes association, mean absolute difference describes score magnitude, and signed bias is OpenSpliceAI minus SpliceAI. Jaccard is the intersection divided by the union of positive calls. The call-count ratio is OpenSpliceAI divided by SpliceAI. Comparator-based MCC and related agreement statistics are not experimental accuracy measures.

The reducer checks missing and duplicate tasks, pair-domain completeness and source identity. Independent real-data-window implementations passed 11,195 primary and 40,020 matched numerical checks. These validate the implemented computations on the tested windows, not biological truth or completeness of unfinished inference. The archived September 9 bundle retains the original engineering narrative and verification evidence.

Annotation context and masking

The proximity index contains 351,042 unique internal boundary coordinates from the scoring annotation. Donor and acceptor roles are assigned by strand. Internal boundaries are pooled across genes, and transcript ends are excluded. We group variants by the distance of the variant coordinate to the nearest indexed boundary: at-site, 1–2, 3–10, 11–50, 51–500 and >500 bp. The main figure pools donor/acceptor context by summing call numerators and denominators before computing rates. The original tables preserve the site-type strata.

The scorer's mask uses a different boundary set: it selects the nearest exon boundary for the specific variant–gene annotation, including transcript ends and pooling donor/acceptor boundary types. A loss is retained only if its selected delta position equals that boundary. A gain at that boundary is suppressed. The mask acts on the selected event position, whereas proximity grouping uses the variant position. This distinction explains why pooled distance and retained loss location must not be interpreted as independent biological labels.

A symmetric 1–2-bp distance group includes both sides of a boundary and is not synonymous with the essential splice dinucleotide. A distance >500 bp does not establish a deep-intronic variant: the index is pooled and no transcript-specific intronic/exonic classification was performed. Far-loss strata may be empty or very small under the mask; eligible counts are needed to interpret their rates.

Current inference progress

The progress collector reads the dated campaign manifests and verifies their checksums, seed labels, complete unique chunk IDs, state totals and provenance totals against the audit metadata. It then compares current input and output file sizes and nanosecond modification times with those recorded in each manifest. Unchanged files retain their audited classification; changed or newly appearing files are unverified, and absent files are missing. An I/O failure cannot produce a valid count. This metadata comparison is deliberately separate from a new VCF content audit.

The scheduler query records matching rs10 and rs13 scoring jobs with their states and reasons. A failed query is reported as unavailable, rather than as no jobs. The displayed observation time and original audit time remain separate. No inference jobs or campaign manifests are changed by this report revision, and no completion date is inferred from a pending job.

Precision and dominant events

The two output formats use different precision: two decimal places for SpliceAI and five for OpenSpliceAI. Rounding OpenSpliceAI to the coarser grid increases exact-score matches, particularly among small scores. Differences exceeding half that grid step, 0.005, remain and support the main score-magnitude comparisons. Exact equality alone is a poor summary when shared zeros are common.

The dominant event is the event with the largest score within a model. Ties and all-zero annotations remain explicit. This summary can expose changes in the largest response, but it compresses four scores and is sensitive to quantization. We therefore retain it as supporting evidence rather than treating dominant labels as mutually exclusive biological outcomes.

Context and selected positions

Survival distributions display the fraction of scores exceeding each cutoff within the stated context group. The large distant population affects total call counts but does not, by itself, increase a within-group rate. The four events differ in both score distributions and retained calling behavior, consistent with the main event-level results.

Conditional score plots retain near and far results for all four events. They show the distribution of OpenSpliceAI scores at a given SpliceAI score, not predictive calibration against observed splicing. Near-boundary gain agreement is closer across much of the score range; this does not imply that every near-boundary gain has equal scores or that the distant calls are false.

Position agreement is conditional on both models crossing the event cutoff. The shared subset may have high positional agreement even when many calls are unique to one model. For retained losses, the common mask constrains both selected positions to the same per-gene boundary. This cannot serve as independent evidence of correct loss localization.

Seed comparisons and genomic heterogeneity

Gene-level method-to-seed ratios extend the matched global comparison. They identify whether the gain–loss contrast occurs across genes rather than only in a global average. Gene-level distributions give each retained gene a contribution, whereas the global means weight annotations. Ratios with a zero seed-difference denominator are undefined and do not become evidence for an infinite effect.

Regional MAX averages provide a broad view of genomic heterogeneity. The revised chromosome figure retains all blocks with at least 10,000 paired annotations and shows their full vertical range. This avoids hiding extreme retained blocks. MAX pools event types, so the plot is secondary to the acceptor/donor gain–loss comparison and does not establish a regional biological mechanism.

Gene-level differences similarly depend on variant composition and retained sample size. The high-divergence genes shown in the original figure are descriptive follow-up candidates, not validated disease associations or an enrichment analysis.
