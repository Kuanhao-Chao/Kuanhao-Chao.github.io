## The question this track answers

The [Statistical Genetics](/deep_dives/statistical-genetics/) track derives the methods. This
one runs them.

That division is real rather than cosmetic. A derivation tells you what an estimator
converges to; it does not tell you which reference panel to use, whether to drop the case or
the control from a related pair, or what a significance threshold costs when you move it.
Those decisions are made in a specific order, most of them before any data is analysed, and
several of them cannot be revisited afterwards. This track is those decisions.

<Callout variant="intuition">

There is one currency in which almost every step of a GWAS can be priced, and it is not
p-values. It is **effective sample size** — the number a power calculation actually consumes,
as opposed to the number of people who consented.

A case-control imbalance reduces it. Relatedness pruning reduces it. Imputation quality
multiplies it. By the time a particular variant is tested, the study that recruited fifty
thousand people may be testing that variant with the power of seventeen thousand, without
anyone having made a mistake. Keeping one running total across the whole pipeline is the
single most useful habit this track teaches.

</Callout>

## The three modules

**Module 1 — Before the Scan.** The decisions that cannot be undone. Who is recruited and in
what ratio, which variants the array types and which the reference panel can fill in, and
which samples and variants survive quality control. The case for doing association studies
this way at all was made on power grounds <Citation id="risch1996future" />, and the same
arithmetic governs every choice in this module.

**Module 2 — The Scan.** Population structure and the association test itself. Structure is
the confounder most likely to be mis-diagnosed, because the inflation factor rises with
sample size under a perfectly clean analysis and the diagnostic that separates the two is
elsewhere <Citation id="bulik2015ldsc" />. The scan is then one small regression repeated ten
million times, wrapped in three choices that change the answer.

**Module 3 — After the Scan.** What the output means and what may be built from it: the
threshold and the biases in the effect estimates, the LD reference that everything downstream
depends on, fine-mapping a locus to candidate variants, and turning the whole thing into a
score. Two decades of results <Citation id="visscher2017gwas" /> have made the architecture
clear — very many variants of very small effect
<Citation id="boyle2017omnigenic" /> — and that architecture is what makes this module's
trade-offs sharp.

## One study, counted nine times

<WorkedExample
  title="Fifty thousand people, and what one variant is worth"
  given={[
    { label: 'Recruited', value: '10,000 cases and 40,000 controls' },
    { label: 'Related pairs', value: '400, broken on the control arm' },
    { label: 'The variant', value: 'imputed at INFO 0.55' },
    { label: 'Threshold', value: '80% power at 5 × 10⁻⁸' },
  ]}
  verifiedBy="src/lib/deepDiveExamples.test.ts"

**Step 1 — the imbalance, from [Study Design](/deep_dives/gwas-study-design/).** Power runs on
a harmonic mean of the two arms:

$$
N_{\mathrm{eff}} = \frac{4}{1/10{,}000 + 1/40{,}000} = 32{,}000 .
$$

Fifty thousand people, thirty-two thousand effective. Balanced recruitment would have given
all fifty thousand, so the ratio cost 18,000 before any data existed.

**Step 2 — the pruning, from [Quality Control](/deep_dives/gwas-quality-control/).** Four
hundred related pairs must be broken. Dropping the *controls* costs

$$
N_{\mathrm{eff}} = \frac{4}{1/10{,}000 + 1/39{,}600} = 31{,}935.48 ,
$$

a loss of 64.52. Dropping the cases instead would have cost 1,032.26 — sixteen times more,
for the same four hundred people. Which member you drop is a decision, and most pipelines
make it by call rate.

**Step 3 — the imputation, from
[Arrays & Imputation](/deep_dives/gwas-arrays-imputation/).** Quality multiplies:

$$
N_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\text{variant}} = 31{,}935.48 \times 0.55 = 17{,}564.52 .
$$

**Step 4 — what that variant can be seen at.** Using $q^2 \geq 39.60/N_{\mathrm{eff}}$,

$$
q^2 \geq \frac{39.600989}{17{,}564.52} = 2.2546 \times 10^{-3} .
$$

The study can detect this variant only if it explains more than about two parts in a thousand
of the trait — while the grant's power calculation, run on fifty thousand people, promised
$7.92 \times 10^{-4}$.

**Step 5 — and none of it was a mistake.** The imbalance reflected who was available. The
pruning was required *by the analysis chosen* — a mixed model would have absorbed the
relatedness instead and skipped that 64.52 altogether. The variant is imputed as well as the
panel permits. Three defensible
decisions, taken in three different lessons, leave $17{,}564.52/50{,}000 = 35.13\%$ of the
headcount at this variant.

The remaining five lessons are about not compounding the problem: not applying genomic
control to a scan that does not need it, not reporting a discovery effect as though it were
unbiased, not fine-mapping with a mismatched panel, and not quoting a fold-change without an
absolute risk.

</WorkedExample>

## What each step decides

The pipeline in order, with the decision each step fixes and what it costs when it is made
badly. Nothing here substitutes for the lesson; it is an index.

<div class="dd-scroll-x">

| Step | The decision it fixes | What it costs when wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Study Design](/deep_dives/gwas-study-design/) | Case definition, ascertainment, the case-to-control ratio | Sets a detection ceiling nothing later can raise |
| [Arrays & Imputation](/deep_dives/gwas-arrays-imputation/) | Which variants exist at all, and how well | A quality filter is a power decision, and it removes rarer variants preferentially |
| [Quality Control](/deep_dives/gwas-quality-control/) | Which samples and variants survive | Differential missingness manufactures associations no other filter can see |
| [Population Structure](/deep_dives/gwas-population-structure/) | Whether the scan is confounded, and what to do about it | Over-correcting destroys real loci; under-correcting invents them |
| [Running the Scan](/deep_dives/gwas-running-the-scan/) | Encoding, covariates, and the model for a binary trait | A mediator covariate removes the effect being measured |
| [Reading the Output](/deep_dives/gwas-reading-the-output/) | Which control — family-wise or false-discovery — and what the estimates mean | FDR on a polygenic trait hands back a catalogue with dozens of wrong entries |
| [LD in Practice](/deep_dives/gwas-ld-reference-panels/) | Which reference panel, and pruning versus clumping | A mismatched panel can make a study eight times too small |
| [Fine-mapping in Practice](/deep_dives/gwas-fine-mapping-practice/) | Which variants are candidates, and how confident to be | A credible set that excludes the truth reports nothing wrong |
| [Building a PRS](/deep_dives/gwas-prs-practice/) | How weights are chosen and how accuracy is reported | Tuning on the test cohort, and fold-changes without absolute risks |

</div>

<Callout variant="key" title="Which column matters">

The third one. Every step in this pipeline runs successfully and produces output whether or
not its decision was right — that is what makes them dangerous. A mismatched LD panel does
not raise an error, an over-corrected scan looks cleaner than a correct one, and a credible
set missing its causal variant reports a *tighter* answer than one containing it — two
variants rather than three, at the same purity. The tooling
<Citation id="purcell2007plink" /> and the published protocols
<Citation id="anderson2010qc" /> will not tell you; each lesson is largely about how to check.

</Callout>

## Exercises

<Exercise n={1} title="Where did the power go?" difficulty="warm-up">

A study recruits 5,000 cases and 45,000 controls — the same 50,000 people as the worked
example, split differently.

(a) What is $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$?

(b) How does that compare with the worked example's 4:1 split?

(c) What would 25,000 and 25,000 have given?

<div slot="solution">

**(a)** $4/(1/5{,}000 + 1/45{,}000) = 18{,}000$.

**(b)** Substantially worse — 18,000 against 32,000, from the same headcount. The 9:1 split is
already at $18{,}000/20{,}000 = 90\%$ of everything controls can ever buy for 5,000 cases, so
almost all of those 45,000 controls are doing nothing.

**(c)** All 50,000. A balanced study is fully efficient, which is why the imbalance is worth
naming as a cost rather than treating as a fact of life: recruiting 20,000 more cases would
be worth far more than the 40,000 controls already collected.

</div>
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} title="Which lesson has the answer?" difficulty="core">

Name the step that owns each problem.

(a) "Our λ is 1.22 and a reviewer wants genomic control applied."

(b) "This locus replicates with an effect half the size."

(c) "Our credible sets got smaller after we tightened an upstream filter."

(d) "The score works in our discovery cohort and not in the clinic's population."

(e) "A variant is significant, has a 98.5% call rate, and sits at Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium."

<div slot="solution">

**(a)** [Population Structure](/deep_dives/gwas-population-structure/). λ cannot separate
confounding from polygenicity; the LDSC intercept can, and applying genomic control on the
strength of λ alone destroys real loci.

**(b)** [Reading the Output](/deep_dives/gwas-reading-the-output/). The winner's curse — a
smaller effect on replication is the expected result for a marginal locus, not a failure.

**(c)** [Fine-mapping in Practice](/deep_dives/gwas-fine-mapping-practice/). Smaller sets may
mean better resolution or may mean the causal variants were filtered out, and coverage and
purity cannot tell you which.

**(d)** [Building a PRS](/deep_dives/gwas-prs-practice/), with the mechanism in
[LD in Practice](/deep_dives/gwas-ld-reference-panels/): accuracy is a property of the
population tested in, and tagging is population-specific <Citation id="martin2019" />.

**(e)** [Quality Control](/deep_dives/gwas-quality-control/). Every filter named is computed
without reference to phenotype and is therefore blind to differential missingness, which is
the one failure mode that manufactures associations.

</div>
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} title="Budgeting a study" difficulty="stretch">

You have 8,000 cases, 32,000 controls, and funding for 8,000 more samples. Typical imputation
quality for the variants you care about is 0.60.

(a) What is the current effective sample size at such a variant?

(b) Spending the 8,000 on cases versus controls: what is each worth at the variant?

(c) A collaborator proposes spending it on deeper sequencing of the existing samples instead,
raising typical INFO from 0.60 to 0.95. Compare.

<div slot="solution">

**(a)** $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 4/(1/8{,}000 + 1/32{,}000) = 25{,}600$, and at INFO 0.60 the
variant is worth $25{,}600 \times 0.60 = 15{,}360$.

**(b)** Adding controls: $4/(1/8{,}000 + 1/40{,}000) = 26{,}666.67$, worth
$16{,}000.00$ at the variant. Adding cases: $4/(1/16{,}000 + 1/32{,}000) = 42{,}666.67$,
worth $25{,}600.00$. Cases are worth $9{,}600.00$ more effective samples than controls at
this variant — the sixteen-fold rule of Module 1, carried through the imputation multiplier.

**(c)** Better imputation raises the variant to $25{,}600 \times 0.95 = 24{,}320$, a gain of
8,960 — nearly as good as spending everything on cases, and it applies to **every variant in
the genome** rather than to the study as a whole. It also raises the INFO of variants
currently below the filter, which adds tests rather than only power. Whether that is the right
call depends on how many variants of interest sit near the threshold, but the comparison is
much closer than it looks, and it is the one most study designs never make.

</div>
</Exercise>

## Summary

- **This track runs the methods the
  [Statistical Genetics](/deep_dives/statistical-genetics/) track derives.** Where a
  derivation is needed it is linked, not repeated.
- **Effective sample size is the currency.** Imbalance reduces it, pruning reduces it,
  imputation quality multiplies it, and keeping one running total across the pipeline is the
  habit that makes the trade-offs visible.
- **Fifty thousand people can be seventeen thousand at a variant** — 35.13% of the headcount —
  with three defensible decisions and no error anywhere.
- **The decisions in Module 1 cannot be revisited.** Ascertainment, ratio and array content
  set a ceiling; everything later is about not falling further below it.
- **Every step produces output whether or not its decision was right.** That is what the
  lessons are for: each one is largely about how to check a step that will not complain.
- **The catalogue <Citation id="sollis2023gwascatalog" /> is the cheapest calibration
  available** for what a study of a given size should expect to find.
