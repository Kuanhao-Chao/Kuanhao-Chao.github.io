## The durable mental model

An LLM endpoint crosses several layers at once: text segmentation, a probabilistic
objective, a Transformer, a decoding policy, and a distributed serving system. Interview
answers become clearer when those layers stay separate.

<Callout variant="intuition" title="A language model is a conditional distribution, not a database">
At each step the model receives a finite token prefix and assigns scores to possible next
tokens. Decoding turns those scores into one continuation. Pretraining changes the score
function; retrieval changes the supplied evidence; sampling changes which continuation is
selected. None of these layers, by itself, verifies that a sentence is true.
</Callout>

## Tokens are the model's units

Subword methods give common strings short representations while retaining a route to rare
ones. Byte-pair encoding became influential in neural translation because it handled rare
words without one word-level unknown token <Citation id="sennrich2016bpe" />. Modern
tokenizers differ in normalization, byte fallback, and special tokens, but the systems
consequence is constant: **billing, context limits, and loss are defined over tokens, not
human-perceived words**.

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-tokenization">
Suppose one tokenizer represents `electroencephalography` as two pieces and another as
eight. The second system spends four times as many positions on the same visible string,
creates more prediction boundaries, and reaches its context limit sooner. This does not
prove its model is worse: a larger vocabulary enlarges the embedding and output matrices,
and token frequency affects how well each piece is learned.

In an interview, name the audit: compare tokens per character and semantic unit across
languages, code, numbers, whitespace, and domain terms. Never truncate by guessed word
counts; call the deployed tokenizer and preserve its exact chat template and special tokens.
</InterviewQuestion>

## Pretraining is likelihood estimation

The chain rule gives

$$
p_\theta(x_{1:T})=\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t}),\qquad
\mathcal L(\theta)=-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t}).
$$

During training a causal mask lets all positions be computed in parallel while each sees
only its permitted prefix. At deployment, selected tokens become future inputs.

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-causal-objective">
The target at position $t$ is $x_t$ and its features come from $x_{<t}$. Teacher forcing
means that even if the model assigns low probability to $x_{t-1}$, training still supplies
the observed $x_{t-1}$ when predicting $x_t$. This makes maximum-likelihood training
parallel and well defined, but inference visits prefixes generated by the model.

The loss is not sentence-level correctness. It rewards probability assigned to every
observed token, including factual and stylistic patterns. Filtering, deduplication, mixture
weights, and contamination therefore define part of the trained behavior.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-masked-vs-causal">
A masked encoder hides selected tokens and predicts them with left and right context,
which is efficient for representation learning. A causal decoder sees only the prefix, so
its conditional matches left-to-right generation. Encoder–decoder denoising can condition
on one sequence while generating another.

Architecture and objective are not synonyms. Ask what attention mask and targets are used.
A bidirectional encoder can score text without being an autoregressive generator; a causal
model can yield embeddings but was not directly trained to make all positions comparable.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-cross-entropy-perplexity">
With $M$ scored tokens, $\bar L=-M^{-1}\sum_t\log p(x_t\mid x_{<t})$ and perplexity is
$\exp(\bar L)$. It is the geometric-mean inverse probability assigned to observed next
tokens—not literally the number of equally likely words.

Do not compare raw perplexities across tokenizers: segmentation changes the events and the
denominator. Bits per byte or a shared text unit helps. Also report domain, packing,
beginning-of-sequence handling, and whether overlapping windows double-count tokens.
</InterviewQuestion>

## Inside a decoder block

The Transformer made attention the main communication operation between positions
<Citation id="vaswani2017transformer" />. In a decoder-only block, causal attention mixes
prefix information, an MLP transforms each position, and residual and normalization paths
support optimization.

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-transformer-path">
For states $H$, projections form $Q=HW_Q$, $K=HW_K$, and $V=HW_V$. A head computes

$$
\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_h}}+M_{\text{causal}}\right)V.
$$

The update returns to the residual stream, followed by a position-wise MLP update. Layer
normalization placement varies; do not memorize “attention then MLP” while forgetting
residual paths. A final vocabulary projection produces logits. Weight tying, grouped-query
attention, rotary positions, and gated MLPs alter details, not this interface.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-context-position">
Content vectors alone do not encode order. Absolute embeddings, relative biases, or rotary
transformations make position available. But accepting $N$ tokens is not using every fact
reliably: training may contain few examples at that length; attention can favor distractors;
evidence may be split, buried, or contradicted.

Evaluate accuracy by context length, evidence position, distractor count, and required
evidence hops. Context capacity is an interface limit, not a reasoning guarantee. This
implementation-sensitive guidance was reviewed 2026-08-22.
</InterviewQuestion>

## Scaling is an empirical planning tool

Kaplan and colleagues fit power-law relationships between autoregressive loss, parameters,
data, and compute <Citation id="kaplan2020scaling" />. Hoffmann and colleagues later found
that, under their setup, model size and token count should grow together
<Citation id="hoffmann2022chinchilla" />.

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-scaling-laws">
Say what was fitted: held-out likelihood under specified data, architecture, optimizer, and
compute conventions. A smooth curve helps choose pilots and reject inefficient allocations.
It does not establish that every downstream capability is smooth, data is interchangeable,
or extrapolation is unbounded.

Thresholded benchmark scores can look abrupt while token probabilities change smoothly.
Report uncertainty in fitted exponents and validate a plan at multiple smaller scales.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-compute-optimal">
Training compute is often approximated as proportional to parameters times tokens, with an
architecture-dependent constant. Under a fixed budget, compute-optimality minimizes
validation loss over both. A larger model trained on too few tokens may lose to a smaller,
better-trained one.

Deployment can reverse the choice: a larger sparse model, smaller dense model trained
longer, and distilled model have different memory and latency. Include data acquisition,
repeated epochs, serving volume, and quality rather than quoting one universal ratio.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-moe">
A router selects one or a few expert MLPs per token. Switch Transformers demonstrated the
capacity benefit and need for load balancing <Citation id="fedus2022switch" />. Activated
FLOPs can stay near a smaller dense model while total parameters rise.

The costs are physical: expert weights occupy memory; tokens move across devices; hot
experts create stragglers; finite capacity can drop or reroute tokens. State whether a
parameter count is total or active, and measure throughput rather than inferring it from
activated FLOPs.
</InterviewQuestion>

## Decoding is a policy over logits

For logits $z$, $p_i(\tau)=\exp(z_i/\tau)/\sum_j\exp(z_j/\tau)$. Holtzman and colleagues
showed that high-likelihood decoding can yield degenerate text and proposed nucleus sampling
to truncate the unreliable tail <Citation id="holtzman2020nucleus" />.

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-decoding">
Greedy decoding commits to $\arg\max_i p_i$ and can miss a better sequence after a locally
less likely token. Beam search retains prefixes by cumulative log-probability, but length
normalization and stopping matter, and likelihood may reward generic output. Sampling
represents diversity at the cost of run-to-run variance.

Match policy to product loss: extraction may value constrained output; brainstorming may
value diverse samples; pass@k deliberately uses repeated attempts. Generation diversity is
not calibrated uncertainty or evidence verification.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-temperature-top-p">
Dividing logits by $\tau$ changes log-odds:
$\log(p_i/p_j)=(z_i-z_j)/\tau$. Low temperature sharpens comparisons. Top-$k$ keeps
exactly $k$ tokens; top-$p$ keeps the smallest probability-ranked set whose cumulative mass
reaches $p$, so its size adapts to distribution shape.

Filter, renormalize, then sample; report the runtime's precise order when combining controls.
Tune against task metrics and malformed-output and safety rates, not a “creativity” label.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-repetition-stopping">
Reproduce with the exact serialized prompt. Verify delimiters, assistant prefix, stop token
IDs, stop strings, output limit, and streaming boundary handling. Inspect token IDs because
visually identical Unicode and whitespace can differ.

If plumbing is correct, compare greedy and sampled traces and inspect repeated
high-probability cycles. Penalties and constraints can mask symptoms and can also suppress
valid repeated code or lists. This runtime checklist was reviewed 2026-08-22.
</InterviewQuestion>

## Serving: prefill, cache, and bandwidth

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-kv-cache">
The cache stores prior keys and values rather than recomputing them. For batch $B$, cached
length $N$, layers $L$, KV heads $H_{kv}$, head width $d_h$, and $s$ bytes per element,

$$M_{KV}=2BNLH_{kv}d_hs,$$

where two means keys plus values. This excludes fragmentation, metadata, workspace, and
weights. Multi-query or grouped-query attention makes $H_{kv}<H_q$, cutting the cache while
leaving query-head count unchanged.
</InterviewQuestion>

<WorkedExample
  title="Budget a KV cache before choosing concurrency"
  given={[
    { label: 'Model', value: '32 layers, 8 KV heads, head width 128' },
    { label: 'Request', value: 'batch 4, cached length 4,096, FP16 cache (2 bytes)' },
  ]}
  verifiedBy="src/lib/mlInterviewExamples.test.ts"
>
$$
2(4)(4096)(32)(8)(128)(2)=2{,}147{,}483{,}648\ \text{bytes}=2\ \text{GiB}.
$$

That is 512 MiB per sequence before weights and workspace. Doubling context doubles this
term. Using 32 instead of 8 KV heads makes it 8 GiB for the batch. Reserve headroom and
load-test fragmentation rather than treating 2 GiB as the whole footprint.
</WorkedExample>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-prefill-decode">
Prefill evaluates prompt positions together, giving efficient matrix operations but large
attention work. It helps determine time to first token. Decode adds one position per active
sequence while repeatedly reading weights and cache; variable sequence lengths complicate
batching. It helps determine inter-token latency and sustained throughput.

Report p50/p95 time-to-first-token, inter-token and end-to-end latency, input/output tokens
per second, queue time, and failures by length. Continuous batching can raise utilization
while worsening one request's tail. These trade-offs were reviewed 2026-08-22.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-attention-complexity">
For all $N$ positions, $QK^T$ and multiplication by $V$ cost $O(N^2d_h)$ per head and a
naive score matrix occupies $O(N^2)$. Projections and MLPs cost roughly $O(Nd^2)$, so
attention need not dominate at short $N$ or large $d$.

With cached decode, one new query compares with $N$ cached positions: that attention step
is $O(Nd_h)$ rather than a new $N\times N$ computation. Many generated tokens still
accumulate work as context grows.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-flash-attention">
FlashAttention changes the memory schedule, not the attention result: tiling and online
softmax avoid writing the full score matrix to high-bandwidth memory
<Citation id="dao2022flashattention" />. Arithmetic remains quadratic for exact dense
attention while auxiliary memory and costly traffic fall.

Do not promise a paper's speedup. Hardware, shapes, dtype, masks, kernels, and surrounding
operations determine end-to-end benefit. This kernel-level statement was reviewed
2026-08-22.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-quantization">
Uniform quantization sketches as
$q=\operatorname{clip}(\operatorname{round}(x/s)+z)$. Lower precision saves weight or cache
bytes and may raise throughput when kernels exploit it. Outliers can dominate scale;
per-channel scales, mixed precision, or calibration help. LLM.int8() isolates outlier
features to preserve quality <Citation id="dettmers2022llmint8" />.

Distinguish post-training from quantization-aware training; weights from activations;
storage from accumulators; and memory savings from measured latency. Validate perplexity,
task, and safety slices. Format and kernel claims were reviewed 2026-08-22.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-llm-inference-capacity">
Start with prompt/output distributions, concurrency, bursts, and streaming. Define
percentile SLOs and a quality floor. Fit weights, cache, and workspace with headroom;
benchmark prefill and decode separately; load-test realistic length mixtures.

Levers include routing, quantization, parallelism, continuous batching, prefix caching,
speculative decoding, admission control, and output caps. Pair every lever with quality and
reliability checks. Peak throughput at intolerable latency does not satisfy an interactive
SLO. This checklist was reviewed 2026-08-22.
</InterviewQuestion>

## Exercises

<Exercise n={1} title="Perplexity and units" difficulty="warm-up">
A model assigns probabilities $[0.5,0.25,0.125]$ to three observed next tokens. Compute
token perplexity. Would it be directly comparable after retokenizing the text?
  <div slot="solution">
    Average negative log-likelihood is
    $-[\log 0.5+\log 0.25+\log 0.125]/3=\log 4$, so perplexity is $4$.
    Retokenization changes events and token count; compare with a common unit and identical
    text handling.
  </div>
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} title="Cache scaling" difficulty="core">
The example moves from batch 4 at 4,096 tokens to batch 6 at 6,144 tokens with other terms
unchanged. What cache memory is required?
  <div slot="solution">
    Multiply 2 GiB by $(6/4)(6144/4096)=2.25$ to get **4.5 GiB**. Runtime overhead and
    weights are additional.
  </div>
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} title="Diagnose a latency regression" difficulty="stretch">
Time to first token is stable but p95 inter-token latency doubles only for long outputs.
Give a focused investigation plan.
  <div slot="solution">
    Stable first-token time makes prefill less likely. Slice by active batch and cached
    length; inspect cache pressure, paging, scheduling, bandwidth, and kernel fallbacks.
    Compare versions with identical length distributions and test whether admission control
    or a cache-format change restores the tail without violating quality.
  </div>
</Exercise>

## Summary

- A causal LM estimates next-token conditionals under a tokenizer; it is not a fact store.
- Scaling, sparse experts, efficient kernels, and quantization are conditional trade-offs,
  not universal guarantees.
- Decoding traverses a learned distribution; it cannot verify unsupported knowledge.
- Production reasoning separates prefill and decode and budgets weights, cache, latency,
  throughput, quality, and cost.
