## The question this lesson answers

The clusters from lesson 9 are numbered, and numbers do not go in a paper. They get names, and
the naming is done with marker genes — usually by looking at a dot plot and asking which
clusters show the marker at all <Citation id="hao2021seurat" />
<Citation id="wolf2018scanpy" />.

Lesson 3 showed why "at all" is the wrong question: a gene at 0.40% of the soup appears in
86.5% of cells that never expressed it, and the rate rises with depth. This lesson asks the
right question — *how much* separation is available between a cell that expresses a marker and
one that does not — and finds that the answer has a ceiling nobody can raise by choosing a
better gene.

## The contrast, and why depth drops out

Carry lesson 3's model forward unchanged. A barcode of depth $n$ holding a cell of type $t$
expects

$$
\lambda_t = n\left[(1-\alpha)\,x_{gt} + \alpha\, s_g\right]
$$

counts of gene $g$ — its own expression, diluted by $(1-\alpha)$, plus its share of the soup.
For a type $A$ that expresses the marker and a type $C$ that does not ($x_{gC} = 0$), the ratio
is

$$
R = \frac{\lambda_A}{\lambda_C}
= \frac{(1-\alpha)x_{gA} + \alpha s_g}{\alpha s_g}
= 1 + \frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\cdot\frac{x_{gA}}{s_g}.
$$

**Depth cancels identically.** It is a ratio of two Poisson means at the same depth, so the
contrast is a property of the biology and the run — not of how deeply any particular cell
happened to be read. Everything that varies with depth in the usual analysis is therefore an
artefact of using a criterion that does not have this property.

## The ceiling, and what it does not depend on

The enrichment $E = x_{gA}/s_g$ looks like the place where a well-chosen marker earns its
keep. It is not, because the soup contains the marker too. Since

$$
s_g = \sum_t \varphi_t x_{gt} \geq \varphi_A x_{gA},
$$

every term being non-negative, we have $E \leq 1/\varphi_A$, with equality exactly when $A$ is
the only type expressing the gene. Therefore

$$
R \leq R_{\max} = 1 + \frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha \varphi_A}
$$

**for every gene in the genome.** And substituting $s_g = \varphi_A x_{gA}$ for a perfectly
specific marker shows $x_{gA}$ cancelling out of the expression algebraically: the marker's own
expression level does not appear in the bound at all.

<WorkedExample
  title="Albumin, and how much more than twelve counts is needed"
  given={[
    'Hepatocytes are 60% of the soup; albumin is 5% of a hepatocyte’s transcripts',
    'Ambient fraction 5%; cells sequenced to 8,000 UMIs',
    'Continuing lesson 3’s exercise, which gives s = 0.03 and λ = 12',
  ]}
  verifiedBy="src/lib/deepDiveExamples.test.ts"
>

**Step 1 — expected counts.** With $s = 0.60 \times 0.05 = 0.03$,

$$
\lambda_{\text{hep}} = 8{,}000\left[0.95 \times 0.05 + 0.05 \times 0.03\right] = 392.0,
\qquad
\lambda_{\text{kup}} = 8{,}000 \times 0.05 \times 0.03 = 12.0.
$$

**Step 2 — the contrast.** $392.0/12.0 = 32.666667$, which equals
$1 + 0.95/(0.05 \times 0.60)$ exactly. In log2 units that is 5.029747 — albumin is a
five-log2 marker of hepatocytes, not an on/off one.

**Step 3 — the cancellation.** Swap albumin for a marker at 0.5% of hepatocyte transcripts:
$s = 0.003$, so $\lambda_{\text{hep}} = 39.2$ and $\lambda_{\text{kup}} = 1.2$. Contrast
32.666667. At 0.05%: 3.92 against 0.12. Contrast 32.666667. Three markers, a hundred-fold
apart in expression, one contrast.

**Step 4 — where the evidence turns.** Under "pure ambient" the cell expects $\lambda_0 = 12$;
under "expresses at albumin's level" it expects $R\lambda_0$. The Poisson log-likelihood ratio
for $c$ counts is $c\ln R - \lambda_0(R-1)$, which is zero at

$$
c^{*} = \frac{\lambda_0 (R-1)}{\ln R} = \frac{12 \times 31.666667}{3.486355} = 108.996353,
$$

so **109 counts** — or, depth-free, **9.083029 times** the ambient expectation. Lesson 3 said a
Kupffer cell would need "substantially more than twelve counts"; this is how much more.

**Step 5 — what presence-and-absence says instead.** At 8,000 UMIs a Kupffer cell shows albumin
with probability $1 - e^{-12} = 0.99999386$, so essentially all of them are "albumin-positive".
At 500 UMIs the same biology gives $\lambda_0 = 0.75$ and 52.76%. **The marker's apparent
specificity is a statement about the sequencing run.**

**The usable statement.** Not presence, but the observed-to-ambient ratio $c/\lambda_0$, whose
null expectation is 1.0 for every cell type at every depth and whose value for a true expressor
is $R$. And $\lambda_0$ is measurable rather than assumed — the empty droplets estimate $s_g$
directly <Citation id="lun2019emptydrops" />
<Citation id="young2020soupx" />.

</WorkedExample>

## Which markers are ruined

The ceiling falls as $\varphi_A$ rises, which produces an awkward asymmetry.

A marker of a cell type making up 1% of the soup has $R_{\max} = 1901$ at a 5% ambient
fraction. A marker of a type making up 60% has $R_{\max} = 32.666667$ — **58.19 times less
headroom**. The genes that work best belong to the rarest types, and the genes that work worst
belong to the type everything else has to be distinguished from.

That is exactly the wrong way round for annotation. Establishing that a small cluster is *not*
hepatocytes, in a liver, is precisely the comparison the ambient soup makes hardest — and it is
the comparison a claim of a novel population rests on. The remedy is not a better gene; it is a
lower $\alpha$, which means correcting the counts before annotating
<Citation id="fleming2023cellbender" />.

<Callout variant="intuition" title="Every cell is a little bit of every cell type">

The mental model that causes the trouble is that a cell's transcriptome is its own. In a
droplet experiment it is its own plus a sample of the tissue's average, and the second part is
not small: at a 10% ambient fraction, a tenth of every count in every barcode came from the
soup.

So the correct null for "does this cell express gene *g*" is never zero. It is $\lambda_0$,
which depends on the gene, the tissue composition and the depth, and which is different for
every gene in the matrix. A dot plot showing that cluster 7 has non-zero mean expression of a
marker is showing you $\lambda_0 > 0$, which was guaranteed before the experiment ran.

The version that carries information is the same plot with the ambient expectation subtracted,
or a ratio against it. Everything in this lesson is a consequence of taking that seriously
<Citation id="luecken2019bestpractices" />.

</Callout>

## Summary

- Under the ambient model the contrast between an expressing and a non-expressing cell is
  $R = 1 + (1-\alpha)E/\alpha$, and **sequencing depth cancels identically**.
- Because the soup contains the marker in proportion to its own type's abundance,
  $E \leq 1/\varphi_A$ and so $R \leq R_{\max} = 1 + (1-\alpha)/(\alpha\varphi_A)$ **for every
  gene**. The marker's own expression level cancels out of the bound.
- For a 60%-abundant type at a 5% ambient fraction, $R_{\max} = 32.666667$, and three markers
  spanning a hundred-fold in expression all realise exactly that: 392 against 12, 39.2 against
  1.2, 3.92 against 0.12.
- Only $\alpha$ and $\varphi$ move the ceiling: 166 / 32.67 / 16 / 7.67 at ambient fractions of
  1 / 5 / 10 / 20 percent, a factor of **21.65**.
- Evidence balances at $c^{*} = \lambda_0(R-1)/\ln R$, which is **9.083029 times** the ambient
  expectation here — depth-free, unlike presence.
- **Markers of abundant types are structurally ruined** — 58.19 times less headroom than a
  1%-abundant type — which is exactly the comparison a novel-population claim depends on.

<Exercise n={1} title="A better marker will not help" difficulty="warm-up">

A collaborator proposes replacing a marker that is 5% of a cell type's transcripts with one that
is 30% — six times stronger — arguing this will separate the type more cleanly. The type is 60%
of the soup and the run has a 5% ambient fraction.

(a) Compute the enrichment $E$ for both markers, assuming each is expressed only by this type.

(b) Compute the contrast for both.

(c) Explain the result in one sentence, and say what would actually help.

<div slot="solution">

(a) For a marker expressed only by this type, $s_g = \varphi x = 0.60x$, so
$E = x/(0.60x) = 1.6667$ for both — the $x$ cancels.

(b) $R = 1 + 0.95 \times 1.6667/0.05 = 32.666667$ for both. Identical.

(c) A stronger marker raises the cell's counts and the soup's counts by the same factor,
because the soup is made of these cells — so both ends of the comparison move together and the
ratio is unchanged. What would help is lowering the ambient fraction: at 1% the ceiling is 166
instead of 32.67, a fivefold gain that no gene choice can match.

</div>

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} title="The same cells, two sequencing runs" difficulty="core">

The same liver sample is processed twice, once to 500 UMIs per cell and once to 8,000, with a
5% ambient fraction and hepatocytes at 60% of the soup. Albumin is 5% of a hepatocyte's
transcripts.

(a) What fraction of Kupffer cells are "albumin-positive" in each run?

(b) What is the contrast in each run?

(c) A reviewer notes that the deep run shows albumin in the Kupffer cluster and asks whether

<div slot="solution">

(a) The ambient expectation is $\lambda_0 = n \times 0.05 \times 0.03$, so 0.75 at 500 UMIs and
12 at 8,000. Detection is $1 - e^{-\lambda_0}$: **52.76%** and **99.999386%** — call it
essentially all of them.

(b) **32.666667** in both. Depth cancels out of the contrast, which is the entire point: the
quantity that changed between runs is the one that carries no information about the cells.

(c) No — or at least, the observation is no evidence either way. Twelve albumin counts is
exactly what a Kupffer cell with no albumin expression is expected to show at this depth, and
the shallow run would have shown the same cells as roughly half albumin-negative. Deciding the
question needs the level against the ambient expectation: a genuine hepatocyte contaminant
would sit near $32.67 \times 12 \approx 392$ counts, and the evidence only begins to favour
expression above $c^{*} = 108.996353$. A cluster averaging twelve is not close.

</div>

</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} title="Why the ceiling is worst where you need it most" difficulty="stretch">

At a 5% ambient fraction, $R_{\max}$ is 32.666667 for a type at 60% of the soup and 1901 for a
type at 1%.

(a) What is the ratio, and what does it mean for a claim of a novel rare population?

(b) Fragile cell types are over-represented in the soup relative to their cell counts. What does
that do to the analysis, and which types does it hurt?

(c) The lesson says the remedy is a lower α rather than a better gene. Give one experimental and
one computational route, and state what each assumes.

<div slot="solution">

(a) $1901/32.666667 = 58.19$. A marker of the rare type separates it beautifully; a marker of
the abundant type barely separates anything. But a novel-rare-population claim is exactly the
assertion that these cells are *not* the abundant type — and that is the comparison with 58
times less headroom. The evidence is weakest precisely where the claim needs it.

(b) The soup's composition is weighted by transcript mass released, not by cell count, so a
fragile abundant type contributes more than its cell fraction — its effective $\varphi$ rises,
and its markers' ceilings fall further. It hurts exactly the types that dissociate badly, which
in most tissues are the large metabolically active ones: hepatocytes, cardiomyocytes, neurons.
Notably these are the same types a global mitochondrial filter removes
<Citation id="osorio2021mito" />, so two independent parts of the pipeline degrade the same
populations.

(c) Experimentally: wash the suspension, or process it faster and colder, so less lysis occurs
before partitioning — this assumes the remaining lysis is unbiased with respect to type, which
it is not, but it reduces $\alpha$ for everything. Computationally: estimate the soup profile
from empty droplets and subtract it <Citation id="fleming2023cellbender" />
<Citation id="stuart2019seurat3" /> — this assumes the ambient profile is the same in occupied
and empty droplets, which fails if capture is size-dependent. Neither is free, and both are
better than choosing a different gene, which provably does nothing.

</div>

</Exercise>
