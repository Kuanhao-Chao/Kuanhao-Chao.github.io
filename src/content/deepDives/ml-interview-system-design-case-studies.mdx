## One framework, different bottlenecks

<Callout variant="intuition" title="The best model is downstream of the design">
A system-design interview is not a contest to name the newest architecture. Clarify the
decision, estimate what must move through the system, and locate the binding constraint. Only
then can labels, model, serving and metrics be right or wrong. Production ML practice repeatedly
finds that data and surrounding software dominate lifecycle effort
<Citation id="amershi2019seml" />.
Making those seams explicit also limits the hidden dependency debt described in deployed ML
systems <Citation id="sculley2015debt" />.
</Callout>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-design-framework">
Use the same visible sequence every time. **Requirement:** user, decision, success, harms,
prediction time, freshness, latency and availability. **Estimate:** QPS, inventory, data,
memory, bandwidth, review capacity and cost. **Architecture:** sources, stores, offline/online
paths and ownership. **Data/model:** label availability, point-in-time features, leakage-safe
split, heuristic baseline and candidate model. **Serving:** batch/stream/online, cache, fallback
and policy. **Evaluation:** offline metrics/slices and causal online test. **Monitoring:** service,
data, prediction and delayed outcomes. **Failure modes:** shift, feedback, abuse, outage and
rollback.

Narrate assumptions and revise them. A design that survives contradiction is stronger than a
dense diagram built on an unstated premise.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-design-recommender">
**Requirement/estimate:** define a meaningful action and long-term satisfaction guardrails;
size users, items, updates, peak QPS and 100-ms-scale budget. **Architecture/data/model:** event
log → point-in-time features → two-tower/heuristic retrieval of hundreds → learned ranking →
policy/diversity re-rank. YouTube's two-stage candidate/ranking architecture is a canonical
example <Citation id="covington2016youtube" />.

**Serving/evaluation:** precompute item embeddings, ANN retrieve, online user context; measure
candidate recall and NDCG/calibration offline, then randomized retention/satisfaction with
guardrails. **Monitoring/failures:** freshness, cohorts, creator exposure, popularity collapse,
position bias, delayed labels and model/index version. Preserve exploration and log propensities;
otherwise the model learns only from items it chose to show.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-design-search">
**Requirement/estimate:** query intent, relevance, language, index freshness, corpus and tail
latency. **Architecture:** ingestion builds lexical and embedding indexes; query understanding
fans into lexical/semantic retrieval; merge, deduplicate, rank and policy-filter. Pairwise
learning-to-rank methods such as RankNet optimize ordering from preferences
<Citation id="burges2005ranknet" />.

**Data/model:** editorial judgments, clicks corrected for position and adversarial spam; begin
with BM25. **Serving/evaluation:** cache normalized head queries carefully; measure recall@K,
NDCG by query class, zero-results and human judgments; interleave/A-B online. **Monitoring/failure:**
index lag, tail query quality, latency, language slices, click feedback and unsafe results. A
semantic ranker cannot recover a document retrieval never surfaced.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-design-fraud">
**Requirement:** minimize expected fraud plus customer friction and review cost, within the
payment deadline. **Estimate/architecture:** peak transactions, feature lookups and investigator
capacity; combine hard rules, event-time account/device/merchant/network features and a model,
routing calibrated risk to approve, review or decline.

**Data/model:** chargebacks are delayed and selective; temporal splits, cost-sensitive objectives
and graph/rule baselines precede complexity. Example-dependent cost modeling makes the decision
objective explicit <Citation id="bahnsen2015fraud" />. **Serving/evaluation:** low-latency store,
strict fallback and shadow; report expected cost, precision at review capacity and calibration.
**Monitoring/failures:** attack adaptation, feature outage, feedback from declined transactions,
cohorts and appeal outcomes. Missing counterfactual fraud on blocked payments is fundamental.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-design-moderation">
**Requirement:** translate policy into severity, locale and action with appeals; estimate upload
and reviewer capacity. **Architecture/data/model:** known-hash/rule filters → multimodal models
and similarity retrieval → calibrated allow/review/block tiers; create adjudicated guidelines,
agreement measures and active-learning queues. Large-scale toxicity work demonstrates both
labeling value and disagreement <Citation id="wulczyn2017toxicity" />.

**Serving/evaluation:** isolate high-severity paths; report policy-specific precision/recall by
language and prevalence plus reviewer workload. **Monitoring/failures:** adversarial evasion,
queue age, policy revision, appeal overturns, coordinated attacks, demographic/dialect impact,
privacy and reviewer welfare. A global threshold that satisfies average accuracy can overload
review or silence one community.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-design-forecasting">
**Requirement:** decision, horizon, hierarchy and asymmetric stockout/holding loss; estimate
series count, cadence and availability. **Data/model:** rolling-origin point-in-time features,
stockout censoring and promotion/calendar inputs; seasonal-naïve baseline before global or
probabilistic models and hierarchical reconciliation.

**Serving/evaluation:** batch quantiles plus explanation of known events; use scaled errors such
as MASE and decision cost because percentage metrics fail near zero. Hyndman and Koehler compare
forecast error measures and motivate scaled alternatives <Citation id="hyndman2006forecastaccuracy" />.
**Monitoring/failures:** bias, interval coverage, inventory outcome, late inputs and regime
change. Do not random-split time or treat unavailable future promotions as features.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-design-anomaly">
**Requirement:** actionable incident classes, time-to-detect and on-call budget; estimate metric
cardinality, stream rate and label delay. **Architecture/data/model:** streaming window/state,
seasonal/robust transforms, rules and forecast or multivariate scores, followed by grouping,
deduplication and routing.

**Serving/evaluation:** replay incidents chronologically and inject realistic faults; measure
precision at alert capacity, coverage, detection delay and false pages per engineer. **Monitoring:**
score/alert distributions, missing telemetry and feedback. **Failures:** alert storms, topology
changes, silent sensors, correlated duplicates and adaptive suppression. Optimize the alerting
decision rather than AUROC over synthetic point anomalies.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-design-medical">
**Requirement:** eligible population, target time, clinical action, benefit/harm and workflow;
estimate sites, decisions and response window. **Data/model:** event-time EHR extraction,
outcome adjudication and missingness semantics; compare a transparent baseline with calibrated
models. Large-scale EHR prediction shows feasibility but not automatic clinical benefit
<Citation id="rajkomar2018ehr" />.

**Serving/evaluation:** interruptible integration, human override and safe downtime; external/
temporal validation of discrimination, calibration, decision curves and subgroups, then prospective
impact study. **Monitoring/failures:** site drift, adoption, alert fatigue, overrides, outcomes,
privacy and automation bias. Predictive accuracy is not proof the intervention improves care.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-design-visual-retrieval">
**Requirement/estimate:** define similarity, rights/safety and latency; size corpus, embedding,
ingest/update and QPS. **Architecture/data/model:** validated media → versioned multimodal
embeddings → ANN index → metadata/filter and learned re-rank. Contrastive image-text pretraining
supports transferable retrieval <Citation id="radford2021clip" />.

**Serving/evaluation:** cache query embeddings, retrieve K and enforce policy; measure ANN recall
against exact search, relevance@K and human judgments by domain. **Monitoring/failures:** index
freshness, embedding/index compatibility, duplicates, unsafe results, demographic/domain slices
and drift. Changing encoder without rebuilding the index silently compares vectors from different
spaces.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-design-rag">
**Requirement:** answerability, citation, ACL, freshness, privacy, cost and abstention. **Estimate:**
documents/churn, chunks, query QPS and context tokens. **Architecture:** parse/version → ACL-aware
hybrid index → retrieve → rerank → context policy → generator → claim/citation checks. RAG jointly
motivates retrieved evidence and generation <Citation id="lewis2020rag" />.

**Data/model:** curated question/evidence sets plus unanswerable and attack cases. **Serving/evaluation:**
measure retrieval recall, context precision, claim support and end-task outcome separately; cache
only with user/ACL/version keys. **Monitoring/failures:** stale documents, permission leakage,
unsupported claims, prompt injection, context conflict, latency/cost and corrections. Generation
quality cannot compensate for missing authoritative evidence.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-design-cold-start">
**Requirement:** acceptable quality/risk for new user, item or entire market. **Estimate:** new
cohort share and evidence acquisition rate. **Architecture/model:** content/context features,
popularity or curated priors, onboarding, transfer and bounded exploration; uncertainty-aware
policy gathers informative feedback.

**Serving/evaluation:** dedicate fallback and exploration slots; measure new-user/item utility,
time-to-personalize and harm, not only mature averages. **Monitoring/failure:** exposure
concentration, sparse-language/region cohorts, manipulation and permanent starvation. The first
policy creates the data for the next; a deterministic popularity default can make alternatives
forever look unsupported.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-design-feedback-loops">
The deployed policy $\pi_0(a\mid x)$ determines exposure, so outcomes for unchosen actions are
missing. Retraining on clicks as iid labels conflates preference with past rank. Simulations show
algorithmic recommendation can homogenize users/items through this loop
<Citation id="chaney2018feedback" />.

**Design:** log candidate set, position, propensity, model/version and downstream outcome;
preserve safe randomized exploration. Off-policy inverse-propensity estimates weight observed
reward by $\pi/\pi_0$, but tiny propensities create variance and zero support prevents
identification. Monitor concentration, creator/group exposure and label coverage. Validate policy
changes online with guardrails rather than declaring an offline counterfactual estimate causal
without overlap assumptions.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-design-launch-experiment">
**Requirement:** primary causal outcome, unit, guardrails, minimum effect and stop conditions.
**Estimate:** baseline rate/variance, power, duration, novelty and interference. **Architecture:**
offline gates → traffic replay/shadow → canary → sticky randomized assignment; log exposure and
versions. Large-scale online experimentation requires trustworthy assignment and diagnostics
<Citation id="kohavi2012experiments" />.

**Evaluation/monitoring:** intention-to-treat primary analysis, predeclared slices, SRM check,
tail latency, harms and long-term indicators. **Failures:** sample-ratio mismatch, network effects,
logging change, peeking, multiple metrics and rollback contamination. Ramp by evidence and keep
the prior path compatible.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-design-estimation">
Estimate what can change the design: daily and peak requests, payload/event volume, retention,
feature/embedding/index storage, bandwidth, model memory, service time and replicas, candidate
pairs per second, label/review throughput and cost. State average versus peak, replication and
overhead. Precision beyond input certainty is theater.

Use estimates iteratively: if exact scoring requires $Q\times M$ comparisons, introduce retrieval;
if the raw index fits memory but replicas do not fit budget, compress or tier it; if review receives
10,000 alerts/day for 500 capacity, threshold/model objective is wrong. Recompute after every
architectural shortcut.
</InterviewQuestion>

Raw vectors require $20{,}000{,}000\times768\times2=30.72$ GB. Three replicas and 30% index
overhead require $30.72\times3\times1.3=119.808$ GB before metadata and allocator reserve.
Retrieval emits $5{,}000\times200=1{,}000{,}000$ query–candidate pairs each second to reranking.

Those two numbers immediately motivate sharded/tiered ANN retrieval and a bounded reranker. They
do not predict p99; benchmark index topology, concurrency and cache distribution.
</WorkedExample>

## Practice

<Exercise n={1} title="Estimate before architecture" difficulty="warm-up">
A service receives 86.4 million requests/day with a 5× peak factor. Estimate average and peak QPS.
<div slot="solution">Average is $86.4M/86{,}400=1{,}000$ QPS; peak is approximately 5,000 QPS.
State whether bursts are sustained and add failover/rollout margin before sizing.</div>
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} title="Retrieval or ranking?" difficulty="core">
NDCG is poor, but the relevant item appears in the top-500 candidates only 55% of the time. Where
should the first experiment focus?
<div slot="solution">Retrieval recall is the binding ceiling: ranking cannot promote an absent
item. Improve/query-slice candidate generation and measure recall@500 first; then evaluate whether
ranking gains remain on fixed candidate sets.</div>
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} title="Selected labels in fraud" difficulty="stretch">
Only approved transactions can later charge back. Why is a model trained on observed outcomes
biased, and what design evidence helps?
<div slot="solution">Approval policy selects which counterfactual outcomes are observed; declined
transactions lack labels. Log policy scores/actions/propensities, use randomized manual review or
limited safe exploration where lawful, and apply causal/off-policy methods only with overlap and
well-defined outcomes. Pure supervised retraining reinforces the existing policy.</div>
</Exercise>

## Summary

- Use requirement → estimate → architecture → data/model → serving → evaluation → monitoring → failure modes.
- Retrieval quality caps ranking; labels are often selected by the deployed policy.
- Offline metrics must connect to causal online decisions and operational constraints.
- Point-in-time availability, fallbacks, human capacity, safety and rollback belong in every design.
- Back-of-the-envelope estimates are valuable when they eliminate an impossible architecture.
