## No labels does not mean no objective

“Find structure” is not a mathematical instruction. Every unsupervised algorithm supplies
its own definition of structure, and its output is meaningful only to the extent that the
definition matches the question.

<Callout variant="intuition" title="The proxy is the product specification">
K-means calls compact Euclidean balls structure. A mixture model calls probability density
structure. PCA calls variance structure. An autoencoder calls reconstructability structure.
t-SNE and UMAP call selected local-neighborhood relations structure. None discovers the
one true organization hidden in a dataset; each preserves some relations and discards
others.
</Callout>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-unsup-purpose-evaluation">
Unsupervised learning removes observed targets, not judgment. The training objective may be
within-cluster distance, held-out likelihood, reconstruction error, contrastive agreement,
or neighborhood preservation. It is a proxy chosen by the designer; optimizing it proves
only that the proxy improved.

Evaluation should triangulate. **Internal:** measure the exact proxy on held-out observations
when it has a probabilistic or reconstructive interpretation. **Stability:** perturb samples,
features, preprocessing, and seeds; compare assignments or neighborhoods after resolving
label permutations. **External:** if trustworthy labels exist but were not used to fit,
compare with the adjusted Rand index, adjusted mutual information, or retrieval quality.
**Downstream:** freeze or
properly refit the representation inside each training fold and test the actual decision.

Avoid selecting an embedding with labels and then claiming label-free discovery. Also ask
whether a stable cluster is actionable and whether important rare structure is being
averaged away. Stability is evidence of reproducibility, not semantic truth.
</InterviewQuestion>

## Centroid clustering

<InterviewQuestion id="q-unsup-kmeans-objective">
K-means solves

$$
J(z,\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^n\lVert x_i-\mu_{z_i}\rVert_2^2.
$$

Lloyd's algorithm alternates two exact conditional minimizers
<Citation id="lloyd1982kmeans" />. With centers fixed, assign each point to its closest
center. With assignments fixed, differentiating the within-cluster sum of squares makes
each center the arithmetic mean. Each step cannot increase $J$; because there are finitely
many assignments, the procedure terminates (up to ties/numerics).

Joint optimization is non-convex, so termination need not be global. Squared Euclidean
distance implies roughly spherical, equal-scale clusters and makes means sensitive to
outliers. Standardize or choose a domain metric before fitting. The arithmetic mean is not
the minimizer for Manhattan distance—that would lead toward medians/k-medoids.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-unsup-kmeans-initialization">
Random initial centers can begin in one dense region, duplicate coverage, create empty
clusters, or converge to a poor local minimum. Multiple independent restarts reduce—but do
not diagnose—the risk.

k-means++ selects the first center uniformly, then samples each new center with probability
proportional to its squared distance from the nearest selected center. It spreads seeds in
the metric the objective uses and provides an expected $O(\log K)$ approximation bound
before Lloyd refinement <Citation id="arthur2007kmeanspp" />.

In code, fix/report seeds for reproducibility but evaluate across seeds for robustness. For
large data, k-means|| or minibatch variants trade approximation for throughput. Empty
clusters can be reseeded to high-error points; silently leaving an empty centroid is an
implementation bug, not a new cluster discovery.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-unsup-choose-k">
An elbow plots compactness against $K$, but compactness must improve as more centers are
added and the bend can be subjective. Silhouette contrasts within-cluster distance with the
nearest other cluster, yet inherits the metric and favors separated compact shapes. Gap
statistics compare compactness with a reference null; likelihood models can use held-out
log likelihood, AIC, or BIC under their assumptions.

The defensible answer combines: a domain range for useful granularity; resampled stability;
an internal diagnostic; held-out or downstream utility; and inspection of tiny/unstable
groups. If the data form a continuum, no $K$ may be intrinsically correct. Report that
rather than forcing ontology onto a partition.

**Trap.** Choose preprocessing and $K$ inside the evaluation design. Picking scaling and
$K$ by the full dataset, then reporting the same score, understates selection uncertainty.
</InterviewQuestion>

## Mixtures, latent assignments, and density shape

<InterviewQuestion id="q-unsup-gmm">
A Gaussian mixture writes

$$p(x)=\sum_{k=1}^K\pi_k\,\mathcal N(x\mid\mu_k,\Sigma_k),\qquad \sum_k\pi_k=1.$$

It returns responsibilities $P(z=k\mid x)$ rather than only a nearest-center label. Full
covariances express elliptical orientation; diagonal/spherical covariances reduce parameters.
K-means emerges as equal spherical variances shrink toward zero and assignments harden.

The flexibility has costs: a full-covariance component estimates $d(d+1)/2$ covariance
terms, and likelihood is unbounded if one covariance collapses onto one data point. Add
covariance floors/priors, multiple initializations, and monitor component mass. A Gaussian
component is a density component—not automatically a semantic population.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-unsup-em">
For latent $z$, observed likelihood contains a difficult $\log\sum_z p(x,z\mid\theta)$.
Introduce any distribution $q(z)$ and use

$$
\log p(x\mid\theta)
=\mathcal L(q,\theta)+D_{KL}\!\left(q(z)\,\|\,p(z\mid x,\theta)\right).
$$

The E-step sets $q$ to the current posterior, making the KL zero and the bound tight. The
M-step maximizes the bound over $\theta$. After the M-step, the new likelihood is at least
the new bound, which is at least the previous likelihood—EM is monotone
<Citation id="dempster1977em" />.

For a GMM, the E-step computes responsibilities; the M-step forms responsibility-weighted
mixture proportions, means, and covariances. Monotonic does not mean globally optimal or
numerically safe: EM can stall at a local solution, converge slowly, or pursue a singular
component. Check likelihood traces, parameter validity, seeds, and held-out fit.
</InterviewQuestion>

<WorkedExample
  title="One GMM responsibility calculation"
  given={[
    { label: 'Mixture priors', value: 'π₁ = π₂ = 0.5' },
    { label: 'Densities at x', value: 'N₁(x) = 0.12, N₂(x) = 0.03' },
  ]}
  verifiedBy="src/lib/mlInterviewExamples.test.ts"
>
The unnormalized component weights are $.5(.12)=.06$ and $.5(.03)=.015$. Normalize:

$$
r_1=\frac{.06}{.06+.015}=.8,\qquad r_2=.2.
$$

This observation contributes weight $.8$ to component 1's mean/covariance update and $.2$
to component 2's. A hard assignment would discard that uncertainty. If prior 1 fell to
$.1$ while prior 2 became $.9$, the same likelihoods would give
$r_1=.012/(.012+.027)\approx.308$: responsibilities combine density and prevalence.
</WorkedExample>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-unsup-clustering-choice">
Match method to the meaning of a cluster.

- **k-means:** fast partitions around Euclidean means; best for compact, similarly scaled
  groups when a hard centroid is useful.
- **GMM:** overlapping elliptical density components, soft membership, and likelihood-based
  prediction; covariance estimation needs data and regularization.
- **Hierarchical:** a dendrogram when nested resolution or inter-cluster relationships
  matter; linkage choice changes the geometry and early merges cannot be undone.
- **DBSCAN:** density-connected shapes and explicit noise without choosing $K$; a single
  radius/min-points pair struggles when densities vary, and high-dimensional distances
  become weak.

First settle metric, scaling, mixed-feature handling, and whether new points need assignments.
Then compare stability and downstream use. Algorithm choice cannot rescue a representation
where “near” has no domain meaning.
</InterviewQuestion>

## PCA as geometry and reconstruction

<InterviewQuestion id="q-unsup-pca-derivation">
For centered covariance $S=X^TX/n$, the variance along a unit direction $v$ is $v^TSv$.
Maximize it subject to $v^Tv=1$. A Lagrange multiplier gives $Sv=\lambda v$; the maximum
is the top eigenvector. Further components impose orthogonality and take descending
eigenvectors, the construction originating with Pearson <Citation id="pearson1901pca" />.

The rank-$r$ reconstruction is $\hat X=XV_rV_r^T$. Its squared error is the sum of squared
discarded singular values, so the top singular subspace also minimizes linear squared
reconstruction error. Compute centered $X=U\Sigma V^T$ directly: it avoids forming $X^TX$
and squaring the condition number.

Shapes: $X$ is $n\times d$, $V_r$ is $d\times r$, scores $XV_r$ are $n\times r$, and
reconstruction returns $n\times d$. Components can flip sign with no mathematical change.
</InterviewQuestion>

<WorkedExample
  title="Variance explained and reconstruction error"
  given={[
    { label: 'Centered rows', value: '(2,0), (0,1), (−2,0), (0,−1)' },
    { label: 'Convention', value: 'S = XᵀX / n' },
  ]}
  verifiedBy="src/lib/mlInterviewExamples.test.ts"
>
$X^TX=\operatorname{diag}(8,2)$, so $S=\operatorname{diag}(2,.5)$. The principal axes
are the coordinate axes with eigenvalues 2 and .5. Keeping the first captures
$2/(2+.5)=.8$, or 80% of variance.

Projecting onto the x-axis reconstructs $(2,0),(0,0),(-2,0),(0,0)$. Only the two vertical
points incur error, one each, so total squared reconstruction error is 2—the discarded
singular value squared. Centering is essential: otherwise the first component can mainly
point toward the mean rather than describe variation around it.
</WorkedExample>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-unsup-pca-traps">
PCA creates components $z_j=Xv_j$, usually dense mixtures of original variables. That is
feature **extraction**, not feature selection. Loadings describe a direction; they are not
regression effects, causal weights, or universally comparable unless preprocessing is fixed.

Because PCA ignores $y$, high variance can represent batch, lighting, scanner, ancestry,
or another nuisance while a low-variance direction carries prediction. Scaling decides
whether physical variance or standardized correlation dominates. Outliers can rotate the
subspace.

Fit centering, scaling, number of components, and PCA on each training fold, transform held-
out data unchanged, and select dimension by downstream validation when the goal is predictive.
A pretty separated PCA plot can reflect leakage or confounding; color was not part of PCA,
but it may have guided analyst choices.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-unsup-whitening">
Let $S=V\Lambda V^T$. PCA whitening maps
$x\mapsto\Lambda^{-1/2}V^T(x-\mu)$, producing identity covariance in the fitted sample.
ZCA rotates back with $V$ after scaling, keeping coordinates closer to the original basis.

Whitening removes second-order scale and correlation, useful for some optimization and
source-separation assumptions. But a tiny eigenvalue receives multiplier
$1/\sqrt{\lambda_j}$: measurement noise in a nearly constant direction can explode.
Use $1/\sqrt{\lambda_j+\epsilon}$ or truncate low eigenvalues, fit only on training data,
and serialize the exact transform.

Identity covariance does not imply independence except under special distributions such as
multivariate Gaussian. Whitening is decorrelation plus scaling, not a proof that features
are independent.
</InterviewQuestion>

## Alternative factorizations and maps

<InterviewQuestion id="q-unsup-ica-nmf">
**PCA** asks for orthogonal variance directions; with only second moments, non-Gaussian
source structure is invisible. **ICA** assumes observations are linear mixtures of
statistically independent, mostly non-Gaussian sources and seeks an unmixing matrix; scale
and order remain indeterminate. Hyvärinen and Oja develop the independence/non-Gaussianity
principle <Citation id="hyvarinen2000ica" />.

**NMF** approximates nonnegative $X\approx WH$ with $W,H\ge0$. Without cancellation by
negative loadings, components can look like additive parts; Lee and Seung demonstrated this
parts-based representation <Citation id="lee1999nmf" />. NMF solutions are non-convex and
non-unique, so initialization and regularization matter.

Choose PCA for best linear squared reconstruction/conditioning, ICA when a plausible
independent-source mechanism exists, and NMF when nonnegative additive factors match the
domain. Component interpretability is an empirical claim, not guaranteed by a factorization
name.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-unsup-tsne-umap">
t-SNE converts high-dimensional neighbor distances into probabilities, chooses low-dimensional
points whose Student-t neighbor probabilities minimize a KL divergence, and uses the heavy
tail to relieve crowding <Citation id="vandermaaten2008tsne" />. UMAP builds a weighted
nearest-neighbor graph from local fuzzy simplicial relationships and optimizes a low-
dimensional graph layout <Citation id="mcinnes2018umap" />.

Both prioritize local neighborhoods. The distance between islands, their area/density, and
sometimes their separation can be artifacts of perplexity/neighbors, minimum distance,
metric, seed, or sampling. Neither is a clustering algorithm or evidence that visible
groups are biological/product categories.

Standardize intentionally, compare seeds and hyperparameters, plot held-out metadata only
after choices are frozen, and quantify any claimed separation in the original space or a
predeclared representation. Use PCA first when speed/noise warrants it, and retain the full
pipeline for new-point transforms where supported.
</InterviewQuestion>

## Learned representations and anomalies

<InterviewQuestion id="q-unsup-autoencoder">
An unconstrained network with enough capacity can copy $x$ through an identity map. Useful
representation pressure comes from a low-dimensional bottleneck, denoising corrupted inputs,
sparsity, contraction, masking, quantization, or another task. Hinton and Salakhutdinov
showed deep autoencoders for nonlinear dimensionality reduction
<Citation id="hinton2006autoencoder" />.

Reconstruction captures information needed to reproduce the input under the chosen loss.
Pixel MSE favors average local appearance; sequence token loss favors token predictability.
Neither automatically captures class semantics. Compare against linear PCA at the same
dimension, evaluate reconstruction on held-out data, probe the frozen representation on
downstream tasks, and test robustness to nuisance factors.

**Trap.** Low reconstruction error can come from memorization. Conversely, a useful invariant
representation may intentionally discard detail and reconstruct worse. The downstream
contract decides which is success.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-unsup-anomaly-detection">
First define “anomaly”: rare under the training distribution, operationally harmful, or
novel enough to review. These are not equivalent. Choose a score whose assumption matches
that definition—distance/density, isolation depth, one-class boundary, predictive residual,
or reconstruction. One-class support-vector methods explicitly estimate a high-density
region boundary <Citation id="scholkopf2001oneclass" />.

Train on a representative, mostly normal historical window when possible, preventing future
and entity leakage. Pick a threshold from investigation capacity and asymmetric cost, not
from a universal contamination fraction. For evaluation, use temporal backtests, delayed
confirmed incidents, realistic fault injection, precision at review budget, time-to-detect,
false-alert burden, and stability across normal regimes. ROC AUC can look excellent when
the useful top-ranked alerts are poor.

Monitor input drift, score distributions, alert composition, feedback delay, and analyst
outcomes. New legitimate behavior can look anomalous; an adaptive update can then learn a
real attack. Human review and controlled retraining are part of the statistical system.
</InterviewQuestion>

## Practice

<Exercise n={1} title="One Lloyd update" difficulty="warm-up">
Points are $0,2,9,11$ on a line, with initial centers $0$ and $9$. Perform one assignment
and centroid-update step. What is the new objective?

<div slot="solution">
Assignments are $(0,2)$ to the first center and $(9,11)$ to the second. Updated means are
$1$ and $10$. The squared-error objective is
$(0-1)^2+(2-1)^2+(9-10)^2+(11-10)^2=4$, down from
$0^2+2^2+0^2+2^2=8$. Reassignment does not change the groups, so this initialization has
converged.
</div>
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} title="PCA can discard the label" difficulty="core">
Feature $x_1$ has variance 100 but is independent of a binary target. Feature $x_2$ has
variance 1 and perfectly separates the target. What happens if PCA keeps one component?

<div slot="solution">
Assuming the features are centered and uncorrelated, PCA keeps $x_1$ because its eigenvalue
is 100 versus 1. It retains about $100/101=99.0\%$ of input variance and discards all target
signal. This is not a PCA bug: the unsupervised objective never saw the target. Choose
dimension within a downstream validation pipeline or use a supervised representation when
prediction is the goal.
</div>
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} title="A persuasive but invalid t-SNE story" difficulty="stretch">
A t-SNE plot shows three islands matching hospital sites. A colleague concludes there are
three disease subtypes. Give a validation plan.

<div slot="solution">
Treat site as a suspected batch variable, not subtype evidence. Refit preprocessing and the
embedding across seeds, perplexities, balanced site samples, and leave-one-site-out data;
check whether neighborhoods persist. Quantify site and clinical-label predictability in the
original/predeclared feature space with patient-aware splits. Inspect acquisition and
processing covariates, correct justified batch effects inside training folds, and seek
replication where subtype labels recur across sites. Island geometry alone supports none of
the causal or clinical conclusion.
</div>
</Exercise>

## Summary

- Every unsupervised method defines structure through a proxy objective; evaluation must reconnect it to use.
- Lloyd's algorithm monotonically improves k-means but initialization and geometry determine the local result.
- A GMM adds probabilistic soft assignments and covariance shape; EM is monotone, not globally optimal.
- PCA's maximum-variance and minimum-reconstruction formulations select the same linear subspace.
- Whitening can improve conditioning but amplifies small-eigenvalue noise and does not establish independence.
- ICA, NMF, t-SNE, UMAP, and autoencoders preserve different relations under different assumptions.
- Anomaly systems require temporal evaluation, review-budget metrics, drift monitoring, and explicit definitions.
