## The first question is whether actions change the future

Reinforcement learning is not “machine learning with a reward.” Its distinctive difficulty
is that an action can change the future states, data, and opportunities used to judge it.
If that pathway is negligible, a simpler contextual bandit—or ordinary prediction—may be
more identifiable and easier to operate.

<Callout variant="intuition" title="Prediction observes labels; decision-making creates them">
A supervised model predicts under a mostly fixed data-generating process. A bandit chooses
which immediate outcome it gets to observe. An MDP also changes what it can encounter next.
Exploration is therefore an intervention with user and safety consequences, not a free
training trick.
</Callout>

## Formulate before optimizing

<InterviewQuestion id="q-rl-bandit-vs-mdp">
Use a contextual bandit when context $x_t$ arrives, action $a_t$ affects immediate reward,
and—conditional on context—the action does not materially change the next context or future
opportunities. Ad selection for one independent visit can approximate this. Use an MDP when
actions affect inventory, user state, information, trust, or later rewards.

Test the causal assumption, not the UI shape. A recommendation can be sequential because it
changes future preferences and exposure; a robot action certainly changes location. If the
state pathway is weak relative to uncertainty, begin with a bandit and long-term guardrails.
If observations omit relevant history, the model may be a POMDP even when called an MDP.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-rl-mdp">
An MDP is $(\mathcal S,\mathcal A,P,R,\gamma,\rho_0)$: states, actions, transition kernel,
reward law, discount (or horizon), and initial-state distribution. A policy maps state to
an action distribution. The Markov claim is
$P(s_{t+1},r_t\mid s_{0:t},a_{0:t})=P(s_{t+1},r_t\mid s_t,a_t)$.

State is therefore a sufficient statistic for controlled prediction—not simply whatever
features are logged. Check time granularity, terminal states, action availability, delayed
rewards, nonstationarity, and whether hidden information requires belief state or memory.
Sutton and Barto provide the standard modern formulation <Citation id="suttonbarto2018rl" />.
</InterviewQuestion>

## Bellman recursion turns a horizon into a fixed point

Bellman's dynamic-programming principle decomposes an optimal continuation into one step
plus an optimal subproblem <Citation id="bellman1957dynamic" />. With
$G_t=\sum_{k\ge0}\gamma^k r_{t+k}$:

<InterviewQuestion id="q-rl-bellman">
For a fixed policy,

$$
V^\pi(s)=\mathbb E_\pi[r_t+\gamma V^\pi(s_{t+1})\mid s_t=s],
$$

because $G_t=r_t+\gamma G_{t+1}$. Conditioning also on action gives $Q^\pi$. Optimality
replaces the next policy average with the best action:

$$
Q^*(s,a)=\mathbb E[r_t+\gamma\max_{a'}Q^*(s_{t+1},a')\mid s,a].
$$

For finite discounted MDPs the Bellman optimality operator is a $\gamma$-contraction, giving
a unique fixed point. Approximation, partial observability, and changing transitions break
the simple tabular guarantee even though the equation remains a modeling target.
</InterviewQuestion>

<WorkedExample
  title="Solve a two-state Bellman system"
  given={[
    { label: 'Policy transition', value: 'A gives reward 2 then moves to B; B gives reward 1 then stays in B' },
    { label: 'Discount', value: 'γ = 0.5' },
  ]}
  verifiedBy="src/lib/mlInterviewExamples.test.ts"
>
The fixed-policy equations are

$$V(B)=1+0.5V(B),\qquad V(A)=2+0.5V(B).$$

Thus $V(B)=2$ and $V(A)=3$. Truncating after the immediate reward would value A at 2 and B
at 1, missing B's continuing stream. With $\gamma=0$, both reduce to immediate rewards;
as $\gamma$ approaches one in a continuing task, return scale and variance grow and finite
value needs appropriate reward/termination conditions.
</WorkedExample>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-rl-value-policy">
Value-based methods estimate $Q$ and choose $\arg\max_a Q(s,a)$, fitting discrete action
spaces and reusing off-policy experience. Policy methods optimize a differentiable
$\pi_\theta(a|s)$ directly, supporting continuous and stochastic actions but often using
high-variance on-policy gradients. Actor–critic uses a policy actor and value critic to
estimate advantages.

Choose by action geometry, observability, simulator/data access, exploration constraints,
off-policy reuse, and required stability. “Model-free” only means no explicit transition
model is used for planning; it does not mean assumption-free or data-efficient.
</InterviewQuestion>

## Value learning and the deadly triad

<InterviewQuestion id="q-rl-qlearning">
Tabular Q-learning updates

$$Q(s,a)\leftarrow Q(s,a)+\alpha[r+\gamma\max_{a'}Q(s',a')-Q(s,a)].$$

The max defines a greedy target policy even when behavior explores, making it off-policy.
Watkins and Dayan proved convergence under tabular coverage and learning-rate conditions
<Citation id="watkins1992qlearning" />. With function approximation, bootstrapping from an
estimate plus off-policy distributions creates the deadly triad and can diverge.

DQN stabilized deep Q-learning empirically with a replay buffer and delayed target network
<Citation id="mnih2015dqn" />. These reduce correlation and moving targets; they do not
remove extrapolation error, coverage failure, max overestimation, or reward misspecification.
</InterviewQuestion>

## Policy gradients trade variance for direct control

<InterviewQuestion id="q-rl-policy-gradient">
For trajectory probability $p_\theta(\tau)$, the log-derivative identity gives

$$
\nabla J(\theta)=\mathbb E_{\tau\sim\pi_\theta}
\left[\sum_t\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)G_t\right].
$$

REINFORCE is this Monte Carlo estimator <Citation id="williams1992reinforce" />. Subtracting
a state-only baseline $b(s_t)$ leaves expectation unchanged because the expected score
function is zero, while lowering variance. Reward-to-go avoids charging an action for prior
rewards. Normalize carefully: changing reward scale changes optimization even when the
optimal policy is theoretically unchanged.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-rl-actor-critic">
An actor update replaces raw return with an advantage estimate, often a temporal-difference
residual $\delta_t=r_t+\gamma V_\phi(s_{t+1})-V_\phi(s_t)$. The critic's bootstrapping
reduces Monte Carlo variance but adds approximation bias. Multi-step or generalized
advantage estimates move along that continuum.

Diagnose actor and critic separately: episodic return and constraints; value loss and
explained variance; advantage distribution; entropy; gradient norms; and sensitivity to
seeds. An inaccurate critic can give confidently wrong directions even while its average
loss falls on a shifted state distribution.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-rl-ppo">
With ratio $r_t(\theta)=\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)/\pi_{old}(a_t|s_t)$, PPO maximizes the smaller of
$r_tA_t$ and a clipped version of $r_t$ times $A_t$ <Citation id="schulman2017ppo" />. The
clip discourages sampled updates that improve the surrogate by moving the ratio far from
one.

It is a heuristic, not a hard trust region or monotonic-improvement guarantee. Multiple
epochs can still move the policy substantially. Monitor approximate KL, clip fraction,
entropy, value error, reward/constraint metrics, and seed variance; stop or adapt when KL
exceeds the intended regime. Correct optimization cannot repair an incorrect reward.
</InterviewQuestion>

## Exploration is an ethical and statistical choice

<InterviewQuestion id="q-rl-exploration">
$\epsilon$-greedy picks a random action with probability $\epsilon$, ignoring structured
uncertainty. UCB chooses an optimistic estimate such as empirical mean plus a confidence
bonus; Auer and colleagues established logarithmic regret for bounded stationary bandits
<Citation id="auer2002ucb" />. Thompson sampling draws a plausible model from a posterior
and acts optimally for that draw, tracing to Thompson's 1933 rule
<Citation id="thompson1933" />.

Guarantees depend on stationarity, reward bounds, model correctness, and feedback. Delayed
rewards, changing catalogs, interference, and unsafe actions alter the problem. Constrain
the action set, start with small traffic, use fallbacks, monitor cohort risk, and never
explore an action that policy forbids.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-rl-contextual-bandit">
Define context available **before** action, feasible actions, immediate reward, delay, and
unit of decision. Choose a policy with explicit exploration, log the full available action
set (when feasible), chosen action, probability, context version, and outcome. Without the
propensity, later counterfactual evaluation loses its foundation.

Start against a strong supervised or rule baseline, replay instrumentation in shadow mode,
then ramp randomized traffic under safety and business guardrails. Handle nonstationarity
with time-aware monitoring or discounting, and distinguish delayed negative outcomes from
missing observations.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-rl-off-policy-evaluation">
For logged $(x_i,a_i,r_i,p_i)$ with $p_i=\mu(a_i|x_i)$, IPS estimates target-policy value:

$$\hat V_{IPS}=\frac1n\sum_i\frac{\pi(a_i|x_i)}{p_i}r_i.$$

It needs consistency, correct propensities, and overlap. Large weights create variance;
self-normalization or clipping trades bias for stability and must be disclosed. Report weight
quantiles and effective sample size.

A doubly robust estimator adds a reward-model prediction and importance-weights its residual;
under standard assumptions it remains consistent if either propensity or reward model is
correct <Citation id="dudik2011doublyrobust" />. Neither repairs unmeasured confounding or
actions with zero logging probability.
</InterviewQuestion>

<InterviewQuestion id="q-rl-reward-failure">
Reward misspecification appears when the optimized metric is only a proxy: a recommender can
increase clicks through sensational content; a robot can exploit a simulator artifact; an
agent can satisfy a checker without completing the intended task. Distribution shift then
lets optimization find loopholes unseen in ordinary validation.

Write a causal theory of how reward connects to value, add side-effect and constraint
metrics, build adversarial environments, hold out exploit families, inspect trajectories,
limit optimization pressure, and keep human escalation. Compare short- and long-horizon
outcomes. A sophisticated policy trained on the wrong reward is a more effective optimizer
of the wrong thing.
</InterviewQuestion>

## Exercises

<Exercise n={1} title="Bandit or MDP?" difficulty="warm-up">
A notification can increase today's click probability but also causes some users to disable
all future notifications. Is a contextual bandit formulation sufficient?
  <div slot="solution">
    Not for the full objective. The action changes future action availability and rewards,
    so state should include relevant notification history or fatigue and the process should
    be modeled sequentially. A bandit can be a baseline only with explicit long-term
    disable-rate guardrails and acknowledgment of myopia.
  </div>
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} title="One Q-learning update" difficulty="core">
Let $Q(s,a)=2$, observed reward $r=1$, $\gamma=0.9$, learning rate $\alpha=0.5$, and
$\max_{a'}Q(s',a')=4$. Compute the update.
  <div slot="solution">
    The target is $1+0.9(4)=4.6$, TD error is $4.6-2=2.6$, and the new value is
    $2+0.5(2.6)=3.3$.
  </div>
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} title="Audit an IPS estimate" difficulty="stretch">
A target policy's estimated lift is driven by three events with importance weights above
100. What do you report and do next?
  <div slot="solution">
    Report weight distribution, effective sample size, uncertainty, and overlap by context;
    verify logged propensities and action sets. Compare direct-model, IPS, self-normalized,
    clipped, and doubly robust sensitivity without hiding bias. Do not launch on the point
    estimate; collect safer randomized coverage in the weak-overlap region.
  </div>
</Exercise>

## Summary

- A bandit assumes actions do not materially change future context; an MDP models that
  action-to-state pathway. Partial observability can invalidate either state representation.
- Bellman recursion links immediate reward to future value; Q-learning, policy gradients,
  actor–critic, and PPO approximate different parts with different bias and data reuse.
- Exploration must be logged, bounded, and safe. Off-policy estimates require overlap and
  trustworthy propensities and can have severe variance.
- Reward is a designed proxy. Validate its causal link to the intended long-term outcome and
  look deliberately for optimization loopholes.
