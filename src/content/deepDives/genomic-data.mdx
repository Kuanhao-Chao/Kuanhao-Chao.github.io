## The question this track answers

Someone hands you a variant: chromosome 17, position 43,106,487, A changed to C. Is it a
problem?

Nothing about that string answers the question. The answer has to be assembled — from a
gene annotation that says which transcript the position falls in, a population database
that says how often the change is seen, a constraint metric that says whether the gene
tolerates damage, a functional assay that says what the change does to the protein, and a
curated archive that says what other laboratories concluded. Each of those lives in a
different resource, with its own coordinates, versions, access rules and characteristic
ways of being misread.

This track is a map of those resources. Not a link list — the value is in what each one
actually measures, how to get it, and where it misleads.

<Callout variant="intuition">
Think of variant interpretation as a court case rather than a lookup.

No single witness decides it. The population database is an alibi witness: it can say "this
change is common in healthy people", which is powerful *exculpatory* evidence and almost
worthless as incriminating evidence. The functional assay is an expert witness: it says what
the change does in a dish, which is not the same as what it does in a person. The clinical
archive is hearsay of a useful kind — it reports what other laboratories concluded, with a
review status telling you how much scrutiny that conclusion received.

The layers are not interchangeable and they are not redundant. Each answers a question the
others cannot, and the interpretation is the combination.
</Callout>

**1 · Reference and annotation** fixes what the variant *is*: an assembly, a coordinate, a
transcript, and a name. Nothing else can happen first. Different transcripts give the same
change different names, which is why <Citation id="morales2022mane" /> exists.

**2 · Population baselines** say how often the change is seen in people not selected for
disease. <Dataset id="gnomad" version /> is the reference, and the approach it inherits was
established by ExAC <Citation id="lek2016exac" />: aggregate enough unselected exomes that
*absence* becomes informative. The layer's whole value is negative evidence, and its
characteristic failure is treating absence as rarity.

**3 · Constraint** aggregates that frequency data up to the gene, asking whether damage to
this gene is tolerated at all <Citation id="karczewski2020" /><Citation id="chen2024constraint" />.
It is a property of the *gene*, not of your variant.

**4 · Functional maps and assays** measure what sequence does — expression, chromatin,
splicing, protein function. Multiplexed assays <Citation id="esposito2019mavedb" /> turn
this from anecdote into a saturating measurement.

**5 · Clinical curation** records what laboratories and expert panels concluded
<Citation id="landrum2024clinvar" /><Citation id="rehm2015clingen" />, under a framework
that says how evidence combines <Citation id="richards2015" />.

**6 · Benchmarks** exist because everything above is now also being predicted by models,
and a prediction is only as good as the evaluation that certified it.

## One variant, all six layers

Every value below was retrieved live from the resource named, on the date given. The
arithmetic is reproducible from the counts shown.

**Layer 1 — what is it called?** The MANE Select transcript for BRCA1 is
`ENST00000357654` / `NM_007294.4` <Citation id="morales2022mane" />. On it the change is
`c.181T>G`, `p.Cys61Gly`. The genomic allele is A→C while the coding change is T→G because
BRCA1 is transcribed from the minus strand — the first place a trace like this goes wrong.
gnomAD annotates this position against **25** BRCA1 transcripts; they agree here, and on
many variants they do not.

**Layer 2 — how often is it seen?** In <Dataset id="gnomad" version />, exomes and genomes
are called separately and reported jointly:

$$
\text{exomes: } \tfrac{25}{1{,}452{,}604} \qquad
\text{genomes: } \tfrac{5}{152{,}214} \qquad
\text{joint: } \text{AF} = \frac{30}{1{,}604{,}818} = 1.8694 \times 10^{-5}
$$

with a 95% Wilson interval of $[1.310, 2.669] \times 10^{-5}$. Rare — and *measured* to be
rare, which is the distinction the next layer down depends on.

**Layer 3 — does the gene tolerate damage?** BRCA1 has 140 observed loss-of-function
variants against 173.66 expected, so $o/e = 0.806$. The published LOEUF is **0.928**, and
you can recompute it: it is the upper bound of the 90% interval on that ratio, which from
those counts is $0.9276$.

$$
\text{LOEUF} = \frac{\text{upper 90\% bound on } o}{e} = \frac{161.08}{173.66} = 0.928
$$

A LOEUF near 1 means **no detectable depletion of LoF variants**, and pLI is
$5.5\times10^{-38}$ — as close to zero as the metric goes.

<Callout variant="warning" title="BRCA1 is not LoF-constrained, and that is not a mistake">
The most famous cancer gene in medicine scores as entirely LoF-tolerant. That is correct,
and it is the single most instructive fact on this page.

Constraint metrics measure **selection in the general population**. BRCA1 heterozygotes are
born, live and reproduce; the cancer risk acts decades later, largely after reproduction.
Selection barely sees it. LOEUF is blind to exactly the class of disease — late-onset,
dominant, incompletely penetrant — that dominates clinical genetics.

So a low LOEUF is evidence a gene matters; a high LOEUF is **not** evidence it does not. And
neither says anything about a specific missense variant, because the metric is computed from
loss-of-function variants only.
</Callout>

**Layer 4 — what does the change do?** Cys61 is a zinc-coordinating residue of the BRCA1
RING domain. Saturation genome editing <Citation id="findlay2018sge" /> measured every
possible single-nucleotide variant across the RING and BRCT domains in their endogenous
context — exon 5, which contains c.181, is inside that window. This is the layer that
supplies experimental rather than inferential evidence.

**Layer 5 — what did others conclude?** ClinVar <Citation id="landrum2024clinvar" /> holds
this as `VCV000017661`, classified **Pathogenic**, review status *reviewed by expert panel*
— the ENIGMA panel, evaluated 2015-08-10. Review status is not decoration: it is the
difference between one laboratory's opinion and a panel's adjudication.

**Combining it.** The ACMG/AMP framework <Citation id="richards2015" /> assigns evidence
strengths, and the Bayesian reading of it <Citation id="tavtigian2018" /> makes those
strengths multiplicative likelihood ratios; the point system <Citation id="tavtigian2020" />
is the arithmetic form. An illustrative tally for this variant:

| Criterion | Evidence | Strength | Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| PS3 | Functional assay shows loss of function | Strong | 4 |
| PS4 | Enriched in affected individuals | Strong | 4 |
| PM2_Supporting | AF = 1.9×10⁻⁵, far below the disease allele frequency | Supporting | 1 |
| PP3 | Computational agreement (REVEL 0.948, phyloP 6.32) | Supporting | 1 |
| | | **Total** | **10** |

With a prior of $0.10$ and one very strong criterion worth 350:1, ten points give

$$
\text{OddsPath} = 350^{10/8} = 1514, \qquad
P = \frac{1514 \times 0.10}{1513 \times 0.10 + 1} = 0.9941
$$

Above the 0.99 threshold: **Pathogenic**, consistent with the expert panel. The criteria
assignment here is illustrative of how the layers combine; the resource values and the
arithmetic are real.

</WorkedExample>

<Callout variant="key" title="What each layer can and cannot do">
- **Frequency is powerful exculpatory evidence and weak incriminating evidence.** A variant
  at 5% in healthy adults is not causing a severe dominant disease. A variant at
  $10^{-5}$ is consistent with pathogenic *and* with being an ordinary rare neutral variant,
  of which every genome carries thousands <Citation id="whiffin2017" />.
- **Constraint is a gene-level property** derived from LoF variants. It cannot speak to a
  missense variant, and it is blind to late-onset disease.
- **Functional assays measure a phenotype in a dish**, chosen by the experimenter. Their
  strength depends entirely on how well that readout was calibrated against known variants.
- **Curation is evidence about evidence.** Its unit of trust is the review status.
</Callout>

## Exercises

<Exercise n={1} title="Absent, or just unobserved?" difficulty="warm-up">

You look up the same variant in a cohort of 2,500 individuals (AN = 5,000) and find no
carriers. Compute the frequency estimate and the upper bound of its 95% Wilson interval.
What can you conclude?

<div slot="solution">

The point estimate is $0/5{,}000 = 0$. The Wilson upper bound is

$$
7.677 \times 10^{-4}
$$

That is **41 times higher** than the frequency measured in gnomAD's 1.6 million
chromosomes. Seeing nothing in 5,000 chromosomes is entirely compatible with a frequency
forty times the true one.

So the conclusion is not "this variant is absent from this population" but "this cohort
cannot measure frequencies below about $10^{-3}$". Absence of evidence, at this sample
size, is very nearly no evidence. That is why ACMG's PM2 criterion is now applied at
*Supporting* strength rather than *Moderate*: the observation is much weaker than its
apparent decisiveness suggests.

</div>
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={2} title="Does a high LOEUF exonerate a gene?" difficulty="core">

BRCA1 has LOEUF 0.928 and pLI ≈ 0 — statistically indistinguishable from a gene under no
constraint on loss of function. A colleague concludes that BRCA1 LoF variants are therefore
unlikely to be disease-causing. Where is the error?

<div slot="solution">

Two errors, one about the metric and one about the disease.

**The metric.** Constraint quantifies depletion of LoF variants relative to a mutational
expectation, i.e. the strength of *purifying selection* on the gene in the general
population. It is an evolutionary measurement, not a clinical one.

**The disease.** Selection acts on reproductive fitness. Hereditary breast and ovarian
cancer is late-onset, incompletely penetrant, and largely post-reproductive. A BRCA1 LoF
carrier's expected number of offspring is close to unaffected — so there is almost nothing
for selection to deplete, and the metric correctly reports almost no depletion.

The general rule: **constraint is informative when it is low, and uninformative when it is
high.** A low LOEUF says damage to this gene is selected against, which is evidence a
disruptive variant matters. A high LOEUF is consistent with a gene that is unimportant *or*
with one whose importance selection cannot see — and those two cannot be distinguished from
the metric alone.

</div>
</Exercise>

<Exercise n={3} title="How much does one criterion move the verdict?" difficulty="stretch">

In the tally above, suppose the functional evidence is judged Moderate rather than Strong —
the assay was not calibrated against enough known-pathogenic controls to justify full
weight. Recompute the total, the posterior and the classification.

<div slot="solution">

PS3 drops from Strong (4 points) to Moderate (2), so the total falls from 10 to 8:

$$
\text{OddsPath} = 350^{8/8} = 350, \qquad
P = \frac{350 \times 0.10}{349 \times 0.10 + 1} = 0.9749
$$

That is below the 0.99 threshold for Pathogenic and above the 0.90 threshold for Likely
pathogenic — the variant is reclassified as **Likely pathogenic**.

Two points to take from this. First, the point system is deliberately steep near the
thresholds: a single step in one criterion's strength is enough to change the reported
class, which is why assay calibration is a first-order concern rather than a technical
footnote. Second, "Likely pathogenic" is not a hedge — it is a specific claim that the
posterior lies in $[0.90, 0.99)$, and it carries the same clinical management implications
as Pathogenic in most guidelines.

</div>
</Exercise>

## Summary

- **Six layers, each answering a question the others cannot.** Identity, then frequency,
  then gene-level constraint, then function, then curation, with benchmarks certifying the
  predictions that increasingly stand in for all of it.
- **The reference layer comes first and is not a formality.** A variant has no name until a
  transcript is chosen, and the same change has different names on different transcripts.
- **Frequency exonerates far more readily than it incriminates**, and a frequency measured
  on a small AN is not a frequency at all — it is an upper bound.
- **Gene constraint is not variant evidence.** BRCA1's LOEUF of 0.928 is correct and says
  nothing about c.181T>G, because selection cannot see a disease that acts after
  reproduction.
- **The layers combine multiplicatively**, and the ACMG point system is the arithmetic that
  makes that combination explicit: ten points is a posterior of 0.994, and losing one step
  of one criterion moves the variant a whole class.
