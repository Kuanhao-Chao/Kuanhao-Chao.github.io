The scientific results remain the September 9 snapshot. The separately dated progress update does not
add newly scored chunks to any scientific comparison. Figures S1–S11 retain the supporting evidence

## Detailed methods

### Scoring collection and models

We used the published masked genic SNV collection distributed with SpliceAI. The collection includes
genic positions and flanking sequence and does not enumerate all possible substitutions at every
reference base. The campaign divides the source into 100,000 chunks. We scored
MANE-trained OpenSpliceAI checkpoints from seeds rs10 and rs13, each using a single checkpoint.
The published SpliceAI scores were produced by a five-model ensemble. The seed labels do not refer to
cross-validation folds. The comparison therefore combines differences in training, checkpoints,
implementation and ensembling.

The recorded setup uses GRCh38.p14, the GENCODE v24 canonical gene table supplied with SpliceAI,
a 10,000-nt model context, a ±50-bp event-search window, and masked output.
Training annotations and scoring annotations have different roles: MANE defines the retraining data,
whereas the campaign's shared canonical gene table controls assignment and masking.
For each event, the score is the largest predicted increase or decrease in the event-search window;
its accompanying delta position records the selected location relative to the variant.

### Audit, pairing and comparison domains

Input and output VCFs were audited for completeness, parseability, source-record preservation and
annotation structure. The analysis pins source artifacts and manifests by checksum and verifies
selected chunk identities. A valid structural audit does not establish the checkpoint that produced
a legacy file. We retain the classification `legacy_unprovenanced` and do not infer embedded provenance
receipts from campaign settings alone.

We pair by chromosome, position, reference allele, alternate allele and exact gene label. We collapse
identical duplicate values and exclude conflicting duplicates. An absent model score is missing, not
zero. Chunk-boundary joining resolves groups split by file partitioning. We retain separate counts
for physical VCF records, distinct variant alleles, gene annotations and paired observations.

### Streaming summaries and numerical verification

The analysis reduces streams of paired annotations to additive counts, moments, fixed-bin
histograms, and stratum tables. It does not materialize a multi-billion-row dataframe. Exact
threshold counters use 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5 and 0.8, with positive calls defined by score ≥ cutoff.
The 200-bin score histograms have width 0.005; conditional quantiles and threshold sweeps derived
from them are approximate. Empty bins remain gaps. Undefined rates remain undefined rather than
being replaced with zero.

All event-level global score summaries use the full paired domain, including shared zeros. Pearson
correlation describes association, mean absolute difference describes score magnitude, and signed
bias is OpenSpliceAI minus SpliceAI. Jaccard is the intersection divided by the union of positive
calls. The call-count ratio is OpenSpliceAI divided by SpliceAI. Comparator-based MCC and related
agreement statistics are not experimental accuracy measures.

The reducer checks missing and duplicate tasks, pair-domain completeness and source identity.
Independent real-data-window implementations passed 11,195 primary
and 40,020 matched numerical checks. These validate the implemented
computations on the tested windows, not biological truth or completeness of unfinished inference.
The archived September 9 bundle retains the original engineering narrative and verification evidence.

### Annotation context and masking

The proximity index contains 351,042 unique internal boundary coordinates from the
scoring annotation. Donor and acceptor roles are assigned by strand. Internal boundaries are pooled
across genes, and transcript ends are excluded. We group variants by the distance of the variant
coordinate to the nearest indexed boundary: at-site, 1–2, 3–10, 11–50, 51–500 and >500 bp. The main
figure pools donor/acceptor context by summing call numerators and denominators before computing rates.
The original tables preserve the site-type strata.

The scorer's mask uses a different boundary set: it selects the nearest exon boundary for the
specific variant–gene annotation, including transcript ends and pooling donor/acceptor boundary
types. A loss is retained only if its selected delta position equals that boundary. A gain at that
boundary is suppressed. The mask acts on the selected event position, whereas proximity grouping
uses the variant position. This distinction explains why pooled distance and retained loss location
must not be interpreted as independent biological labels.

A symmetric 1–2-bp distance group includes both sides of a boundary and is not synonymous with the
essential splice dinucleotide. A distance >500 bp does not establish a deep-intronic variant: the
index is pooled and no transcript-specific intronic/exonic classification was performed. Far-loss
strata may be empty or very small under the mask; eligible counts are needed to interpret their rates.

### Current inference progress

The progress collector reads the dated campaign manifests and verifies their checksums, seed labels,
complete unique chunk IDs, state totals and provenance totals against the audit metadata. It then
compares current input and output file sizes and nanosecond modification times with those recorded
in each manifest. Unchanged files retain their audited classification; changed or newly appearing
files are unverified, and absent files are missing. An I/O failure cannot produce a valid count.
This metadata comparison is deliberately separate from a new VCF content audit.

The scheduler query records matching rs10 and rs13 scoring jobs with their states and reasons. A
failed query is reported as unavailable, rather than as no jobs. The displayed observation time and
original audit time remain separate. No inference jobs or campaign manifests are changed by this
report revision, and no completion date is inferred from a pending job.

### Precision and dominant events

The two output formats use different precision: two decimal places for SpliceAI and five for
OpenSpliceAI. Rounding OpenSpliceAI to the coarser grid increases exact-score matches, particularly
among small scores. Differences exceeding half that grid step, 0.005, remain and support the main
score-magnitude comparisons. Exact equality alone is a poor summary when shared zeros are common.

The dominant event is the event with the largest score within a model. Ties and all-zero annotations
remain explicit. This summary can expose changes in the largest response, but it compresses four
scores and is sensitive to quantization. We therefore retain it as supporting evidence rather than
treating dominant labels as mutually exclusive biological outcomes.

## Seed comparisons and genomic heterogeneity

Gene-level method-to-seed ratios extend the matched global comparison. They identify whether the
gain–loss contrast occurs across genes rather than only in a global average. Gene-level distributions
give each retained gene a contribution, whereas the global means weight annotations. Ratios with a
zero seed-difference denominator are undefined and do not become evidence for an infinite effect.
