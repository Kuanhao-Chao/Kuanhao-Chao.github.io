## 1. Biological motivation and evidence tradeoffs

Genome annotation lift-over asks a deceptively difficult question: given a structured annotation on a reference assembly, what is the most defensible representation of that annotation on a target assembly? The input is not merely a list of intervals. A protein-coding gene contains a hierarchy of parent and child features, one or more transcript models, ordered exons, phased coding segments, untranslated regions, attributes, and identifiers that downstream software may treat as stable keys. A useful transfer must therefore recover content, place it coherently on the target sequence, retain plausible exon and coding structure, and emit a valid graph of GFF3 relationships. Recovering many gene names while corrupting parentage or reading frame is not success; producing an elegant protein match while discarding non-coding hierarchy is not a complete annotation transfer.

Two evidence types expose different parts of this problem. DNA-to-DNA alignment is strong when local sequence identity and synteny are preserved. It can carry untranslated sequence, introns, non-coding features, and reference organization through the transfer. Its weakness is that nucleotide divergence, assembly rearrangement, large indels, and repeated sequence can fragment or misplace the path. Protein-to-genome alignment discards synonymous variation and is naturally aware of splice junctions and reading frame. It can remain informative after nucleotide evidence becomes weak, but it does not by itself preserve the full reference hierarchy and may find paralogous or otherwise biologically inappropriate loci. Neither source is a universal oracle.

LiftOn was designed around that complementarity. Liftoff, backed by minimap2, supplies a DNA-anchored annotation model. Miniprot supplies an independent splice-aware protein model. LiftOn aligns the implied translations to the reference protein, combines compatible segments, repairs open reading frames when doing so improves the protein, and selects among candidate outcomes conservatively. A later rescue pass can add coding genes that the DNA lift missed. The biological premise is not that protein evidence should overwrite DNA evidence. It is that disagreement between two imperfect signals can be resolved locus by locus, with explicit safeguards and a reference protein as a common scoring coordinate.

This report evaluates that implementation premise at a release boundary. It does not repeat the original paper's broader method-development study, and it does not ask whether every transferred locus is independently known to be correct in the target. Instead, it asks four operational scientific questions.

1. **Comprehensiveness:** how much of the reference annotation is retained, including coding transcripts, all counted feature types, and stable exon/CDS identifiers?
2. **Accuracy and structural plausibility:** how well do recovered coding models retain the reference protein and internal coding structure, and do those comparisons remain favorable on a common recovered set?
3. **Practicality:** what wall time and command-level maximum RSS are required for controlled endpoint work and for complete end-to-end runs?
4. **Integrity:** are outputs valid and deterministic, and does the exact released tag satisfy every prespecified qualification gate?

Those questions require different measurements. Coding completeness and all-feature completeness measure recovery, not accuracy. Mean protein identity among recovered transcripts measures sequence agreement but can improve merely because difficult transcripts were omitted. Coverage-weighted protein identity (CovPI) assigns an implicit zero to unrecovered reference coding transcripts and therefore couples quality to recovery. Exact intron-chain and ORF-valid recall add structural constraints. Common-set protein identity holds the compared transcript set fixed between versions. Structural validation and byte-level determinism address still different failure modes. No single bar can stand in for all of them.

The interpretation is correspondingly narrow. “Accuracy” below means **reference-content retention and sequence/structure plausibility**. The `canonical-v1` campaign has no truth annotation for target loci, no experimentally verified orthology labels, and no independent evidence that a selected locus is the biological counterpart rather than a plausible paralog. A high protein score is necessary evidence for many coding transfers, but it is not proof of target-locus truth. A future truth-aware campaign can test that question; no such sealed result is available for v1.0.11, so none is implied here.

### 1.1 Evidence layers and headline conclusion

The report maintains a strict boundary between two evidence layers.

- **Exact-release diagnostic:** v1.0.11 commit `c623f0bddc5b5051a3670a3b0064c52cc61bb719` is compared directly with v1.0.8 commit `e503643d8346c600fedabcd3a4dff5c0873a4a37`. These controlled subset, controlled whole-genome, and end-to-end results determine the qualification verdict.
- **Historical cross-tool context:** a frozen 17-genome comparison includes Liftoff, miniprot, v1.0.8, and a functionally measured LiftOn arm at `7fc84192d85044c946eee5b90e0683eed722f9ea`. It answers a useful comparative biology question, but it is not relabeled as an exact v1.0.11 execution and is never pooled with release statistics.

The exact-release result is diagnostic, not a PASS. Aggregate wall time, memory, completeness, and protein-retention results are often favorable, but 28 controls are absent because v1.0.8 fails deterministically on seven cases; the observed end-to-end panel violates several lower-confidence-bound quality gates; two worst-cell performance caps fail; and one successful exact-tag whole-genome output has a reproducible cross-sequence parent defect. Favorable averages cannot cancel a missing control or a structural validity failure. The report therefore remains unlisted and `noindex`, available for direct technical review without being promoted as a passing release claim.

## 2. Exact v1.0.11 patch and cumulative engineering since v1.0.8

Version numbers matter scientifically because a patch release and a cumulative endpoint comparison answer different historical questions. The exact v1.0.11 patch is small. The v1.0.8→v1.0.11 comparison is much larger. This section separates them before discussing algorithms or measurements.

### 2.1 What v1.0.11 itself changed

v1.0.11 is a **single-fix release** for duplicate-ID ingestion. GFF3 permits real-world files that contain repeated feature identifiers even though those identifiers complicate database construction. Gffutils can transform a duplicate ID by appending a numeric suffix. LiftOn v1.0.10 replaced the default fallback to make this transformation deterministic, but the generated `<id>_<n>` name could itself collide with a feature already created through Liftoff's copy convention. Rice supplies a compact example of the collision class; two additional registered inputs expose the same mechanism.

The v1.0.11 fix narrows the repair. Only a repeated ID whose generated suffix is already occupied moves into a `_dup<n>` namespace. The code distinguishes malformed input handled by the fallback from programming defects raised inside the fallback, allowing internal exceptions to propagate instead of being reclassified as annotation errors. That is the patch-release claim. Concurrent aligners, the third candidate outcome, adaptive rescue, gene-like hierarchy detection, transactional output, and most performance work did not originate in this patch and must not be attributed to it.

### 2.2 Cumulative endpoint differences

The qualification nevertheless compares exact v1.0.11 with v1.0.8, so it observes all intervening engineering. The cumulative changes fall into algorithmic behavior, reliability, and optional execution paths.

| Capability | v1.0.11 endpoint behavior | Default or qualification status |
|---|---|---|
| Concurrent external evidence | Liftoff/minimap2 and miniprot can execute concurrently | default; `--serial-aligners` opts out |
| Bounded protein alignment | Anchor-windowed alignment avoids unbounded traceback matrices | default; legacy reproduction remains available |
| Conservative outcome selection | Compares chained, DNA-anchored, and eligible direct-miniprot models | default; compatibility flags can narrow candidates |
| ORF rescue | Repairs candidate coding boundaries only when the score improves | default |
| Adaptive miniprot-only rescue | Adds genuinely missing coding genes after the anchored pass | default; disabled in controlled panels |
| Gene-like hierarchy handling | Detects pseudogene and ncRNA-like top-level parents as well as `gene` | default; `--gene-only` narrows scope |
| Deterministic locus parallelism | Bounded workers with stable reassembly and publication order | thread-dependent execution path |
| Streaming/in-memory ingestion | Reduces evidence-file and database materialization | opt-in execution paths |
| Content-addressed annotation cache | Rebuilds stale or corrupt parser databases from bound inputs | default |
| Transactional output | Stages, validates, records a run manifest, then atomically publishes | mandatory baseline gate |

The controlled subset and whole-genome panels deliberately disable miniprot-only rescue in v1.0.11 because v1.0.8 has no equivalent pass. Those panels isolate the shared post-alignment problem. End-to-end runs retain output-affecting candidate defaults and therefore measure deployable behavior, including rescue. The distinction prevents a recovery feature from being mistaken for a pure engine speedup or for a like-for-like completeness comparison.

Some execution features are explicitly outside the primary claim. `--stream`, `--inmemory-liftoff`, and native compatibility hooks can reduce intermediate materialization, but the campaign does not present them as the universal default path. Native capability metadata records what was available; it does not imply that minimap2 or miniprot was silently replaced. This report describes the paths actually frozen in controller plans.

### 2.3 Post-tag work is not exact-release evidence

Commit `c8daa44` follows the v1.0.11 tag and repairs cross-sequence duplicate-parent/child binding in disk and in-memory emitters. It is directly relevant to the structural defect found by qualification, but it is a different candidate SHA. Its attempted follow-up lane is not substituted for, averaged with, or presented as the exact v1.0.11 result. The same v1.0.8 control failures terminate the relevant missing pairs, so that lane also does not create a completed replacement campaign. The scientific record is clearer when the exact released artifact remains visible and the repair is described as subsequent engineering.

## 3. Algorithmic and execution methods

### 3.1 Version-specific pipeline

The method map below is specific to the cumulative v1.0.8→v1.0.11 endpoint. It leaves the v1.0.9 report's visualization unchanged. Each step is server-rendered for no-JavaScript and print use; the controls only change visual emphasis.

<LiftOnV111Pipeline />

Reference preparation begins by indexing the annotation and discovering top-level gene-like structures. A literal `gene` filter is insufficient for production RefSeq and Ensembl-derived files: pseudogenes, non-coding RNA genes, and other parent types may own transcripts or exons without using that exact feature type. LiftOn recognizes parents by their hierarchical role, extracts transcript and protein sequences, and retains the source attributes required to reconstruct the target annotation.

Liftoff and miniprot then produce complementary evidence. Their external computations do not depend on each other, so concurrent execution reduces elapsed time when host policy permits. Concurrency does not merge their semantics. Liftoff's result remains the DNA-anchored model; miniprot's result remains protein-to-genome evidence. The locus stage brings them together only after both are normalized into comparable transcript structures.

### 3.2 Protein-maximization chaining

Two models can encode different exon boundaries yet translate over overlapping reference-protein intervals. LiftOn aligns each implied translation to the full reference protein and projects coding-segment endpoints into cumulative reference-amino-acid coordinates. Points at which both candidate paths have consumed the same reference interval delimit comparable groups. Within each group, LiftOn retains the source with higher partial protein identity; exact ties select Liftoff, preserving the DNA-anchored structure. Concatenating selected groups creates a hybrid model that can correct a local error without replacing a stronger region elsewhere.

<LiftOnChaining mode="published" />

The visualization is illustrative rather than measured benchmark data. It reproduces the published algorithmic logic: align full translations, project boundaries, form equal-endpoint groups, score partial proteins, select each group, and concatenate. This local comparison is why a protein-derived splice can repair one segment while the remainder of a reference-organized DNA lift is retained.

### 3.3 Three outcomes, ORF rescue, and conservative selection

The current endpoint evaluates up to three outcomes for a coding transcript. The first is the protein-maximized DNA/protein chain. The second is the original Liftoff structure. The third is a clean CDS model derived directly from miniprot. The third model is eligible only when its strand agrees and it satisfies structural prerequisites; it must strictly improve the rescued protein score to displace the two-way winner. That condition protects against adopting a protein alignment merely because it exists.

ORF rescue examines candidate coding boundaries and reading frames for a better open reading frame. A repaired model must improve the reference-protein comparison beyond the configured threshold. The selection unit is an outcome after eligible rescue, not a raw source alignment. The best-of-outcome rule therefore prevents an attempted merge or repair from becoming mandatory. When evidence is equal, deterministic tie-breaking favors the conservative DNA-anchored path.

This design offers a bounded claim of non-regression under its scoring function: introducing another candidate does not require emitting it when a stronger existing candidate is available. It does not guarantee target-locus truth, because all candidates may point to the wrong locus or share an upstream evidence error. It also does not guarantee every structural metric improves, because protein identity is not identical to intron-chain agreement. That distinction motivates separate qualification gates.

### 3.4 Adaptive missing-gene rescue

The anchored locus pass cannot evaluate a reference gene that Liftoff missed entirely. A later miniprot-only pass considers those coding genes. Its acceptance floor adapts from 0.50 toward 0.30 as DNA-lift recall declines, reflecting the fact that a more divergent transfer supplies fewer anchored candidates. Reference-ID guards prevent a gene already represented by another outcome from re-entering as an untracked copy, and overlap checks suppress incompatible duplications.

Rescue is additive and protocol sensitive. It can increase coding recovery, but it also changes the set of scored transcripts and the amount of work. For this reason it is disabled only in the controlled endpoint panels and retained in the end-to-end panel. Historical default-rescue experiments appear solely in the appendix protocol that produced them. No release statistic pools rescue-disabled and rescue-enabled cells.

### 3.5 Hierarchy reconstruction and transactional publication

Output construction must preserve graph semantics as well as coordinates. Coding transcripts are normalized to `mRNA`; non-coding transcript types retain their appropriate type. Rebuilt CDS segments carry deterministic IDs across discontinuous rows. Parent attributes are rewritten through a single allocation path so that copies, duplicate inputs, and rescued models refer to the target hierarchy rather than stale reference objects. Gene-like parents allow pseudogene and non-coding hierarchies to survive even when their type is not literally `gene`.

LiftOn writes to a staged output, performs an always-on baseline structural pass, records phase, input, version, resource, and failure information in `lifton_output/run_manifest.json`, and publishes atomically only when the gate succeeds. Qualification adds semantic and target-bound validators that are more demanding than ordinary publication. The baseline gate checks syntax, identifiers, ordering, and essential relationships; it is not claimed to prove every parent and child share a sequence or every target coordinate is valid. That precise gap is important because the exact-tag defect passed ordinary execution but failed qualification.

### 3.6 Parallelism and determinism

Independent loci can be evaluated with bounded workers. Completion order is nondeterministic, but emission order need not be. Results carry their submission index, completed work waits in a bounded structure, and only a contiguous prefix is flushed. Stable tie-breaking, identifier allocation, and publication order then make a threaded run comparable with serial execution. The campaign verifies bytes, semantic feature multisets, transcript score tables, and end-to-end biological summaries rather than accepting this design argument on trust.

Bounded protein alignment attacks a different scaling problem. Unrestricted dynamic-programming traceback can grow with the product of long sequence lengths. Anchor-windowed alignment constrains the work while retaining local regions necessary for candidate scoring. Streaming miniprot ingest and in-memory Liftoff transfer can further reduce temporary files and databases. These mechanisms explain why runtime and RSS require separate measurement: a method can use less memory without running faster on every cell, and shared-host I/O can obscure endpoint improvements.

### 4.3 Three panels answer different questions

The controlled subset panel uses cached Liftoff and miniprot evidence and a single-threaded endpoint to expose post-alignment behavior on tractable slices. It includes same-species, cross-species, stress, format, and divergence cases. Because aligners are outside the timed endpoint and rescue is disabled, this panel is appropriate for regression localization but not for estimating an ordinary whole run.

The controlled complete-genome panel also reuses frozen evidence, disables candidate-only rescue, and executes the v1.0.11 locus path and v1.0.8 legacy path with eight endpoint threads. It tests whether scaling changes survive complete annotation graphs, long genes, and genome-wide identifier pressure. Five planned cases have no successful v1.0.8 control, leaving 12 observed case summaries.

The end-to-end panel rebuilds DNA and protein evidence from registered genome and annotation inputs. Both arms use the fastest supported source-aware mode with eight threads, while v1.0.11 retains its output-affecting defaults. Bee, human, and mouse produce four successful pairs each. Arabidopsis and rice lose all control repetitions to the historical duplicate-ID lookup defect. End-to-end results therefore describe the three observed animal cases and cannot be generalized to the two omitted plant inputs.

### 4.4 Accuracy and comprehensiveness metrics

Coding completeness is the number of recovered reference coding transcripts divided by the reference coding denominator. All-feature completeness aggregates the registered feature census after excluding types that the evaluator designates non-transfer targets. It is sensitive to hierarchy scope: recovering pseudogenes or non-coding RNA parents can improve it even if coding completeness is unchanged.

Mean protein identity is calculated among recovered coding transcripts with a valid comparison. It describes the average recovered model but does not penalize omission. CovPI divides the sum of recovered protein identities by the full reference coding denominator, assigning zero contribution to unrecovered transcripts. Identity-threshold recall similarly counts reference coding transcripts recovered at or above PI thresholds 0.50, 0.75, 0.90, and 0.95.

Exact intron-chain recall compares strand-aware internal CDS boundaries after removing dependence on absolute target coordinates. A recovered model succeeds only when the chain matches the reference structural pattern. ORF-valid recall requires a start methionine, terminal stop, and no internal stop. Both use the reference coding denominator, so absence remains a failure. Common-set mean PI restricts both versions to coding transcripts recovered by both and reports the candidate-minus-reference average, reducing selection bias from unequal recovery.

Stable-ID preservation is evaluated separately for CDS and exon identifiers. For discontinuous CDS rows, the evaluator recognizes the reference identifier namespace rather than treating every segment as an independent biological transcript. Preservation is not the same as biological correctness, but it matters operationally for downstream joins, database updates, and reproducible comparisons.

### 4.5 Performance statistics and gates

Wall and maximum-RSS ratios are paired within repetition as v1.0.11/v1.0.8. Repetition ratios are medianed within a benchmark case. Panel summaries use the geometric mean across cases, with a deterministic, seeded, 10,000-replicate bootstrap over case-level units. Confidence intervals therefore reflect heterogeneity across datasets, not variation across independent computers. A panel with fewer than two cases would be descriptive only.

Total-work wall ratio divides the sum of candidate cell medians by the sum of reference cell medians. It weights long cells more heavily than the geometric mean and is reported beside, not substituted for, the case-balanced statistic. Worst-cell ratios enforce a separate practical cap. GNU-time maximum RSS is a command statistic, not a synchronized sample of a process tree. The command-RSS slot-sum proxy adds the largest candidate command measurements that could occupy the recorded concurrency limit and compares that engineering proxy with a 192 GiB admission gate.

Prespecified performance gates require wall and RSS geometric-mean upper bounds and total-work ratios at or below 1.10, worst-cell wall and RSS ratios at or below 1.25, and the concurrency proxy at or below 192 GiB. Quality lower confidence bounds must remain above −0.001 for panel-level metrics. Common-set and per-cell caps provide additional protection. All candidate outputs must pass current validation and remain deterministic. The complete immutable campaign check is itself blocking for a release-mode PASS.

### 4.6 Corrected evaluation overlay

The initial evaluator treated coding GTF `transcript` rows and standards-compliant GFF3 `mRNA` rows as different container classes. On the Ensembl-format case, LiftOn legitimately harmonized coding transcripts to `mRNA` while retaining non-coding `transcript` rows, but the old scorer interpreted the type change as zero coding recovery. That was an evaluator artifact, not evidence that all 2,371 coding transcripts disappeared.

Three immutable, panel-scoped overlays rescore every accepted pair with `mRNA` and coding `transcript` in one transcript namespace. The overlays do not alter output, controller state, timing, or validation. They rehash their inputs, use one evaluator implementation, cover each selected pair exactly once, and reject mixed tooling. Final quality tables use this corrected evidence. The stale zero-recovery value is never presented as a biological result.

### 6.2 Recovered-set identity versus CovPI

Mean protein identity among recovered coding models rises slightly for all three observed end-to-end genomes. That is useful but incomplete. Human v1.0.11 recovers 387 fewer coding transcripts than v1.0.8, and mouse recovers 116 fewer. When those omissions contribute zero through CovPI, the direction reverses. ### 6.3 Intron-chain and ORF plausibility

Protein identity can be high despite a shifted splice boundary, especially when the change is short or preserves amino acids. Exact intron-chain recall therefore asks whether the internal CDS-boundary pattern agrees after removing absolute coordinate dependence. ORF-valid recall asks whether the recovered coding sequence begins with methionine, terminates with a stop, and contains no internal stop. These tests are closer to structural biology than a raw identity average, but they still compare with the reference model. Target-specific alternative splicing or genuine gene disruption could make the reference structure inappropriate.

The controlled panels improve both measures. End-to-end exact intron-chain recall falls in all three aggregate contributions enough for the lower confidence bound to fail; ORF-valid recall also breaches the panel floor. Bee's candidate ORF-valid value is slightly higher while its exact chain is slightly lower. Human and mouse lose on both absolute recall measures. The mixed pattern argues for retaining separate structure endpoints rather than treating ORF repair as proof of splice correctness.

### 7.2 Wall-time heterogeneity and failed worst-cell gate

The full-genome aggregate does not describe every complete genome. `arabidopsis_to_rice` has a median wall ratio above one and a high repetition-level coefficient of variation. `t4_drosophila_to_bee` reaches the panel's worst median ratio, 1.819, failing the 1.25 worst-cell cap even though its repetition geomean and individual timings are strongly affected by host variability. This difference between a case-balanced geometric mean and a work-weighted total is informative. A user running one small dataset should not expect the total-work speedup; a service processing a mix resembling the measured corpus may care about it.

AB/BA repetition controls reduce a fixed order effect but cannot remove shared-host contention. The controller watcher recorded load and transient uninterruptible-I/O states during active epochs. Candidate and reference run close together inside each pair, yet a burst can still affect one arm disproportionately. The bootstrap summarizes datasets, not host episodes. Performance numbers should therefore be read as contention-aware throughput evidence on one machine, not as an isolated hardware benchmark.

### 7.3 RSS, concurrency, and proxies

The v1.0.11 endpoint's largest controlled command measurements are far below the enormous legacy peaks seen in problematic genes. Bounded alignment and reduced materialization explain the direction. A few subsets have RSS ratios near or above one because the v1.0.8 absolute baseline is already small. `candida_albicans_to_dubliniensis` supplies the subset worst RSS ratio, 1.273, narrowly failing the 1.25 cap. That local failure is retained even though the subset RSS geometric mean is strongly favorable.

GNU Time reports the maximum resident set of the wrapped command according to operating-system accounting. Child-process treatment, short peaks, and overlapping commands make it unsuitable as a literal host-demand trace. The slot-sum proxy deliberately errs toward a simple admission calculation: take the largest candidate command RSS contributors up to the concurrency limit and sum them. It can overstate demand when peaks do not coincide and understate it when process-tree accounting misses children. It is an engineering guard, not a physical-memory measurement.

## 8. Validity, determinism, and qualification verdict

### 8.1 Exact-tag structural defect

Candidate outputs pass current validation in 48 of 49 observed logical cells. The exception is the successful complete-genome `arabidopsis_to_rice` cell. Its output binds `rna-DA397_mgp31` on target sequence `NC_029264.1` to a parent gene on `NC_011033.1`. That creates both cross-sequence parentage and a target-bound consistency violation. The defect reproduces across the sealed repetitions and belongs to exact candidate SHA `c623f0b`.

The ordinary run can exit successfully because baseline streaming validation does not perform every deep target-bound relationship check. Qualification does. This is not evidence that baseline validation is useless; it is evidence that its documented boundary matters. A transactional write gate prevents many malformed files from publication, while a release audit can still discover a semantic relationship outside that gate. The post-tag `c8daa44` repair is described in Section 2.3 but is not allowed to rewrite this exact-tag outcome.

### 8.2 Determinism

All 49 observed candidate cells are byte-deterministic, semantic-multiset deterministic, quality-table deterministic, and common-set-score deterministic across four repetitions. End-to-end biological summaries are also deterministic. The result covers different execution orders and threaded complete-genome paths. It supports the claim that bounded parallel completion is reassembled stably.

Determinism is necessary but not sufficient. A reproducible cross-sequence parent defect is still a defect, and a deterministic quality regression still fails its gate. Likewise, two versions can be individually deterministic while differing in biologically meaningful ways. The campaign keeps byte, semantic, score, validity, and quality outcomes separate so that one form of reproducibility cannot mask another.

## 9. Discussion, limitations, reproducibility, and historical appendix

### 9.1 What the results support

The endpoint comparison supports three engineering conclusions. First, the cumulative v1.0.8→v1.0.11 path dramatically reduces command RSS on many difficult loci and complete genomes while improving aggregate wall time. Second, broader gene-like hierarchy handling raises all-feature completeness and stable-ID retention without a controlled-panel coding-recovery collapse. Third, protein-aware selection and ORF repair improve CovPI, threshold recall, common-set identity, and structural recall in the controlled panels.

The end-to-end result qualifies all three. On bee, the candidate is almost neutral in coding recovery and slightly favorable across several quality measures. On human and mouse, broader feature retention and recovered-set mean identity improve while coding recovery, CovPI, and structural recalls decline. Because the observed E2E sample is three cases, and because two plant controls fail, these cells cannot establish a general default-rescue superiority claim. They are a lower-bound warning that endpoint gains do not automatically transfer to every full pipeline outcome.

The validity result is similarly instructive. Transactional baseline validation and deterministic publication remove broad classes of partial or order-dependent output. They did not detect one cross-sequence relationship checked by qualification. The appropriate response is layered validation and explicit validator scope, not a claim that successful exit is equivalent to a biologically coherent annotation.

### 9.2 Limitations

1. **One non-exclusive host.** The campaign uses paired order reversal and bounded admission but not dedicated hardware. Background computation, filesystem contention, cache state, and I/O stalls can affect ratios. The measurements estimate throughput in the observed environment, not a portable hardware constant.
2. **Several concurrency epochs.** Subset, full, and end-to-end continuations use different recorded ceilings. Each panel's selected roots remain visible in provenance. Panel results are not pooled across protocol types, but within-panel host conditions can still vary.
3. **Reconstructed host metadata.** Source, plans, inputs, external annotation tools, and Python dependencies were frozen before execution. The final CPU, RAM, kernel, and GNU Time descriptions were reconstructed at finalization and should not be treated as continuous telemetry.
4. **Command-level RSS.** GNU-time maximum RSS and the slot-sum proxy are reproducible wrapper statistics, not synchronized host-memory measurements. They support relative engineering decisions, not precise capacity planning.
5. **Deterministically missing controls.** Five controlled complete genomes and two end-to-end plants have no v1.0.8 pair. The missingness is mechanistically informative and distinctly non-random. Observed ratios should not be extrapolated to those cases.
6. **Corrected scoring is post-run.** The evaluator overlay repairs transcript-type equivalence and rehashes inputs. It cannot repair output, change timing, or turn a failed arm into a pair. It validates reduction, not execution.
7. **Reference content is not target truth.** Completeness, PI, CovPI, intron-chain, ORF, and stable-ID metrics all depend on the reference annotation. Genuine target-specific loss, gain, alternative splicing, or rearrangement can make perfect retention biologically wrong.
8. **Current validation is still layered.** The qualification validator catches the exact-tag cross-sequence parent defect that baseline publication misses. Neither validator establishes orthology or experimental expression.
9. **Rescue changes the candidate population.** Controlled panels disable v1.0.11-only rescue; E2E enables it. Results answer different questions and cannot be numerically pooled.
10. **Very-distant transfer remains recall-limited.** DNA anchoring loses sensitivity when synteny collapses. Protein-only rescue narrows but does not erase that gap, and permissive protein evidence increases paralogy risk.
11. **Historical external comparisons are protocol-limited.** Liftoff and miniprot are measured broadly in the frozen appendix. LiftOver and CAT feasibility observations were sparse and prerequisite-dependent, so they are not promoted into a ranking.
12. **No sealed `canonical-v2` truth results exist.** Truth-aware synthetic, orthology-backed, or curated-locus experiments are future work only. This report does not preview, infer, or simulate their outcome.

### 9.6 Future truth-aware evaluation and conclusion

A stronger biological evaluation would bind transferred models to independently supported target loci. Synthetic rearrangements could provide exact source-to-target truth; curated ortholog sets could test paralog discrimination; high-confidence target annotations could measure locus, transcript, splice, and copy precision/recall. Such a campaign also needs explicit handling of true lineage-specific loss and gain so that reference retention is not automatically called correct.

Those experiments are reserved for a truth-aware `canonical-v2` profile. They are future work because no sealed `canonical-v2` result exists. This report neither forecasts a PASS nor borrows target-truth language for `canonical-v1` plausibility metrics.

The current evidence distinguishes exact-release behavior, historical comparisons, and contention-aware throughput. All reported values are generated from the immutable result documents, with missing or failed control cells retained as explicit limitations. The diagnostic conclusion is deliberately conservative: cumulative v1.0.11 engineering improves aggregate efficiency, broad annotation retention, stable identifiers, and most controlled protein/structure metrics, but the exact release is not qualified as a PASS. Incomplete deterministic controls, end-to-end lower-bound failures, shared-host performance outliers, and the exact-tag structural defect remain visible. That evidence boundary is more useful than a simpler success narrative because it tells users which gains are measured, which limitations are mechanistic, and which biological questions remain unanswered.
