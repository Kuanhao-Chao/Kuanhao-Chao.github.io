## 1. Biological motivation and evidence tradeoffs

Genome annotation transfer asks more than whether a sequence can be aligned. A eukaryotic annotation is a graph of genes, transcripts, exons, coding segments, untranslated regions, non-coding features, attributes, and stable identifiers. Each feature must be placed on a target assembly while preserving strand, phase, parentage, ordering, and the intended biological relationship among rows. A program can recover a familiar gene name yet break its coding frame, attach a transcript to a parent on another sequence, or discard the non-coding hierarchy. Conversely, it can find a plausible protein elsewhere in the genome while missing the orthologous locus. A useful evaluation therefore has to separate recovery, molecular plausibility, agreement with an external target annotation, file integrity, and computational cost.

DNA and protein evidence illuminate different failure modes. DNA-to-DNA alignment is strongest when local identity, order, and synteny are conserved. It can carry introns, untranslated regions, non-coding RNAs, regulatory-style features present in the source annotation, and the relationships among them. Its sensitivity falls when nucleotide divergence, rearrangement, large indels, repeats, or assembly differences fragment the path. Protein-to-genome alignment ignores synonymous change and can detect splice-aware coding structure after nucleotide evidence weakens. It is correspondingly less informative for non-coding content and more vulnerable to selecting a paralog with a convincing amino-acid match.

LiftOn is built around the complementarity of these signals. Liftoff, using minimap2, supplies a DNA-anchored model. Miniprot supplies protein-to-genome evidence. LiftOn compares the implied translations with the reference protein, can combine compatible portions of the two structures, attempts open-reading-frame repair, and conservatively selects among candidate outcomes. A later rescue stage can recover coding genes absent from the DNA-anchored pass. The central biological proposition is not that protein evidence should overwrite DNA evidence. It is that two imperfect signals can be reconciled locus by locus while retaining the source hierarchy and using the reference protein as a common scoring coordinate.

This report tests that proposition at an exact software-release boundary. It asks five questions:

1. **Comprehensiveness:** how much source annotation is retained, for coding transcripts and for the broader feature hierarchy, and are exon and CDS identifiers preserved?
2. **Source-relative accuracy:** how well do recovered coding models retain the source protein, intron pattern, and open reading frame when absence remains a penalty?
3. **Released-target concordance:** among explicitly matched ortholog groups, how closely do the lifted gene loci, transcript loci, splice structures, CDS structures, and proteins agree with a high-quality annotation released for the exact target assembly?
4. **Practicality:** what elapsed time and sampled process-group memory are required to generate both DNA and protein evidence and publish the annotation?
5. **Integrity:** do all outputs pass structural and target-bound checks, reproduce across four paired repetitions, and satisfy every prespecified cohort gate?

These questions require multiple denominators. Mean protein identity among recovered transcripts can rise when difficult models disappear. Coverage-weighted protein identity (CovPI) addresses that selection effect by assigning no contribution to an unrecovered source coding transcript. Exact intron-chain recall and ORF-valid recall add structural requirements. Common-recovered-set comparisons hold the transcript set fixed between releases. Target-annotation scores introduce a second biological perspective, but only inside a frozen ortholog scope. Stable-ID preservation, GFF3 validity, determinism, wall time, and memory each describe a different operational property. No single score is allowed to stand in for all of them.

“Accuracy” in this report consequently has a bounded meaning: **retention of source content and concordance with an assembly-matched released target annotation**. The target annotation is substantially more informative than source retention alone because its coordinates and structures were generated on the target assembly. It is still not an independent experimental oracle. RefSeq and source annotations can share protein, homology, RNA, or database evidence; a published annotation can contain omissions or errors; alternative isoforms may be represented differently; and even a reciprocal best hit can obscure paralogy or lineage-specific duplication. Agreement is evidence consistent with correctness and completeness, not proof of the uniquely correct biological locus.

### 1.1 Evidence layers and reporting boundary

Two evidence layers are kept visibly separate.

- **Exact-release cohort.** LiftOn v1.0.11 commit `c623f0bddc5b5051a3670a3b0064c52cc61bb719` is compared directly with LiftOn v1.0.8 commit `e503643d8346c600fedabcd3a4dff5c0873a4a37`. Seven transfers are fixed before execution. A transfer enters aggregate biological and performance statistics only after both versions complete all four paired repetitions. A failed planned transfer remains an explicit incomplete outcome and prevents a cohort PASS; it is never silently removed.
- **Historical cross-tool context.** A frozen 17-genome experiment compares Liftoff, miniprot, v1.0.8, and a functionally measured LiftOn arm at `7fc84192d85044c946eee5b90e0683eed722f9ea`. That experiment supplies useful context across a wider divergence range, but it is neither an execution of the exact v1.0.11 tag nor part of the new confidence intervals or gates.

The seven-transfer verdict is conjunctive. Completion, validity, determinism, source-retention non-regression, target-annotation concordance, elapsed-time behavior, and memory behavior must all satisfy their prespecified thresholds. A favorable average cannot cancel a failed transfer, invalid hierarchy, target-concordance regression, or extreme performance outlier. Even a cohort PASS would not retroactively qualify every genome pair supported by the release. Earlier exact-tag evidence exposed missing historical controls and a cross-sequence parent defect; it remains diagnostic evidence and is not pooled with this study. The report is therefore unlisted and `noindex`, available by direct URL for technical review without a blanket release claim.

## 2. Exact v1.0.11 patch and cumulative engineering since v1.0.8

Version attribution matters. The patch that created v1.0.11 is small; the endpoint difference between v1.0.8 and v1.0.11 is much larger. The experiment measures the endpoint difference, so this section separates the two before interpreting results.

### 2.1 The v1.0.11 duplicate-ID ingestion fix

v1.0.11 is a focused fix for duplicate-ID ingestion. Real GFF3 files sometimes repeat feature identifiers despite the complications this creates for database construction. Gffutils can transform a repeated identifier by appending a numeric suffix. LiftOn v1.0.10 replaced the default fallback to make that transformation deterministic, but the generated `<id>_<n>` name could itself collide with an identifier already produced by Liftoff's copy convention. The collision was observed in rice and in additional registered annotations.

The v1.0.11 repair is deliberately narrow. When a repeated identifier's ordinary generated suffix is already occupied, the feature moves into a `_dup<n>` namespace. The fallback also distinguishes malformed input from an exception raised by the callback implementation, so an internal programming error is not incorrectly presented as an annotation problem. That behavior is the patch-release claim. Concurrency, outcome selection, ORF repair, rescue, generalized hierarchy handling, transactional publication, and most performance work predate the patch and must not be described as additions unique to v1.0.11.

### 2.2 Cumulative endpoint differences

The exact comparison against v1.0.8 observes all intervening engineering:

| Capability | v1.0.11 endpoint behavior | Interpretation in this study |
|---|---|---|
| Concurrent external evidence | Liftoff/minimap2 and miniprot can run concurrently | Reduces elapsed evidence-generation time when host policy permits |
| Bounded protein alignment | Anchor-windowed work avoids unbounded traceback matrices | Targets pathological memory growth while retaining scored regions |
| Conservative outcome selection | Compares chained, DNA-anchored, and eligible direct-miniprot models | May change structure and protein agreement at a locus |
| ORF rescue | Adjusts coding boundaries only when the protein score improves | May improve coding plausibility without changing the chosen locus |
| Adaptive missing-gene rescue | Adds eligible coding genes missed by the anchored path | Can improve recovery but can also admit paralogous models |
| Gene-like hierarchy recognition | Handles pseudogene and ncRNA-like parents as well as literal `gene` | Expands non-coding and noncanonical feature retention |
| Deterministic locus parallelism | Uses bounded workers with stable result reassembly | Affects speed while preserving publication order |
| Streaming and in-memory paths | Reduce selected intermediate materialization | Capability is probed; unsupported flags are never sent to older code |
| Content-addressed annotation cache | Rebuilds stale parser databases from bound inputs | Prevents an old database from masquerading as current evidence |
| Transactional output | Stages, validates, records a manifest, then publishes atomically | Prevents a partial file from appearing as a successful result |

The present study runs the deployable pipeline from a source annotation and two genome assemblies. v1.0.11 retains the output-affecting behavior its exact tag supports, including adaptive rescue. v1.0.8 receives only options its CLI actually advertises. This is therefore an endpoint comparison, not a factorial ablation. If v1.0.11 recovers more features, runs faster, or uses less memory, the result supports the cumulative endpoint but does not identify a single causal commit. Likewise, a regression cannot automatically be assigned to rescue or concurrency without a dedicated ablation.

Before that comparison, a separate exact-v1.0.11 canary runs representative insect and primate transfers through the fastest supported path and a conservative path. Semantic feature content, score summaries, target concordance, validation results, and biological summaries must agree; byte hashes are retained diagnostically. The canary protects the scientific comparison from treating an unvalidated execution shortcut as equivalent merely because both commands exit successfully.

### 2.3 Post-tag work is outside the measurement

Commit `c8daa44` follows the v1.0.11 tag and repairs cross-sequence duplicate-parent/child binding in disk and in-memory emitters. It is scientifically relevant because earlier qualification found that defect, but it is not exact v1.0.11. Neither the source worktree used for the candidate nor any report reducer substitutes this commit for the tag. Describing the repair is useful; counting its behavior as v1.0.11 evidence would be incorrect.

This boundary also applies to report tooling. Reducers and validators may be developed after the tag to measure frozen outputs, provided they bind and hash their inputs and never mutate or relabel the candidate. The LiftOn executable and imported package for each arm come from clean detached worktrees at the two stated SHAs. The working development tree, including post-tag changes, is not on either arm's import path.

## 3. Algorithmic and execution methods

### 3.1 Version-specific pipeline

The diagram below describes the cumulative v1.0.11 endpoint. It is separate from the visualization in the v1.0.9 report, which remains unchanged. Keyboard controls alter emphasis only; the complete method is present in the server-rendered markup and printable caption.

<LiftOnV111Pipeline />

Reference preparation indexes the annotation, identifies parents by hierarchical role, extracts transcript/protein sequences, and retains reconstruction attributes. This permits pseudogene and non-coding parents to own children without requiring the literal type `gene`.

Liftoff supplies DNA-anchored structures; miniprot independently aligns source proteins to the target genome. They can run concurrently, but remain distinct until the locus stage normalizes them for comparison. Concurrency changes scheduling rather than evidence semantics.

### 3.2 Protein-maximization chaining

Two candidate models can disagree in exon boundaries while translating over overlapping intervals of the same reference protein. LiftOn aligns each implied translation to the complete reference protein and projects coding-segment endpoints into cumulative reference-amino-acid coordinates. Points at which both paths have consumed the same reference interval delimit comparable groups. Within each group, the source with higher partial protein identity is retained; an exact tie favors Liftoff, preserving the DNA-anchored structure. Concatenating selected groups yields a hybrid model that can repair a local region without replacing stronger evidence elsewhere.

<LiftOnChaining mode="published" />

The visualization is algorithmic, not a measured locus. A common protein coordinate permits local comparison despite intron and assembly differences. Deterministic grouping remains source-protein dependent and cannot establish locus orthology.

### 3.3 Three outcomes, ORF rescue, and conservative selection

The endpoint evaluates up to three outcomes for a coding transcript. The first is the protein-maximized chain. The second is the unmodified DNA-anchored Liftoff model. The third is a clean CDS model derived directly from miniprot. The third outcome is eligible only when strand and structural prerequisites are satisfied, and it must strictly improve the rescued protein score to displace the two DNA-anchored alternatives. This rule avoids adopting a protein alignment simply because it exists.

ORF rescue tests eligible boundary and frame adjustments and retains one only after sufficient protein-score improvement. Selection occurs afterward, with ties favoring the DNA-anchored model. This protects the selection score but cannot guarantee better structure or locus placement: all outcomes can share an upstream locus error or disagree with the target release's isoform.

### 3.4 Adaptive missing-gene rescue

The anchored locus pass cannot evaluate a source gene that Liftoff misses completely. A later miniprot-only pass considers absent coding genes. Its acceptance floor adapts from 0.50 toward 0.30 as DNA-lift recall falls, reflecting reduced anchored sensitivity at greater divergence. Reference-ID guards prevent a gene already represented through another outcome from re-entering as an untracked copy, and overlap checks suppress incompatible duplications.

Rescue can change recovery, protein agreement, runtime, and paralogy risk. It remains enabled as deployable endpoint behavior, but the study provides no causal rescue-only estimate.

### 3.5 Hierarchy reconstruction and transactional validation

Output construction preserves graph semantics as well as intervals. Coding transcripts normalize to `mRNA`; non-coding types remain appropriate; discontinuous CDS rows receive deterministic identifiers; and one parent-allocation path keeps copies, duplicate source identifiers, and rescued models in the published hierarchy.

LiftOn stages output, performs an always-on structural pass, records provenance and failures in `lifton_output/run_manifest.json`, and publishes atomically after the baseline gate. Qualification adds semantic, hierarchy, target-bound, and determinism checks. These establish machine integrity, not orthology or expression.

### 3.6 Parallelism, resource sampling, and determinism

Independent loci can be processed by bounded workers. Completion order is nondeterministic, but emission order need not be. Each result carries a submission index, completed work waits in a bounded structure, and only a contiguous prefix is flushed. Stable tie-breaking, identifier allocation, and final ordering make a threaded result reproducible in principle. The experiment verifies that premise by comparing bytes, semantic feature multisets, transcript score tables, target-annotation summaries, validation results, and biological summaries across four repetitions.

Bounded protein alignment addresses a different scaling risk: unrestricted dynamic-programming traceback can grow with the product of long sequence lengths. Anchor-windowed alignment limits this work while retaining the local regions needed for scoring. Streaming miniprot ingestion and in-memory Liftoff paths can further reduce temporary materialization when the exact source supports them. These mechanisms motivate separate elapsed-time and memory measurements. A path may lower memory without accelerating every dataset; concurrency may lower elapsed time while increasing simultaneous residency.

Memory is sampled for the complete spawned process group once per second and reported as the sum of resident-set size across observed processes. This captures children that a wrapper-command maximum can miss. It can double-count shared pages and can miss a peak shorter than the sampling interval, so it is explicitly a process-group proxy rather than unique physical memory. Four paired repetitions and a two-transfer concurrency proxy make the measurement useful for comparative engineering without presenting it as an exact host-capacity requirement.

## 4. Biology-driven experimental design

### 4.1 Why these seven transfers

The study was redesigned around successful, complete genome annotation mapping rather than around small slices or a heterogeneous collection of execution modes. Seven source–target pairs were fixed before the release comparison. Together they span three biologically useful regimes while remaining close enough for a structured annotation transfer to be a defensible task.

**Honey bee assembly transfer.** Amel_HAv3.1 is mapped to ASM1932182v1, a chromosome-level assembly of another *Apis mellifera* line. Holding species constant makes coordinate conversion, chromosome naming, copy handling, and hierarchy preservation prominent. The comparator is the annotation published with the target assembly; its chromosome labels are mapped to verified INSDC accessions in the target FASTA.

**Fruit-fly species transfer.** *Drosophila melanogaster* to *D. erecta* DereRS2 is compact enough to expose transcript differences clearly while introducing intron turnover, protein divergence, and lineage-specific content. NCBI Annotation Release 101 provides an assembly-matched comparator without mammalian annotation scale obscuring the result.

**Human to western lowland gorilla.** This close-primate comparison preserves broad synteny while retaining assembly gaps, duplications, and isoform differences. NHGRI_mGorGor1-v2.1_pri and its RefSeq release test rich hierarchy retention, stable identifiers, and the distinction between protein agreement and transcript agreement.

**House mouse to *Mus caroli*.** CAROLI_EIJ_v1.1 with RefSeq Release 101 provides a non-primate mammalian comparison. Rapidly evolving rodent loci and a source annotation richer than the target expose recovery and concordance under asymmetric curation.

**Dog to domestic cat.** F.catus_Fca126_mat1.0 with RefSeq Release 105 adds moderate carnivore divergence and veterinary relevance. Gene-family expansion, segmental duplication, and alternate transcript representation make explicit ortholog scoping important.

**Human to rhesus macaque and common marmoset.** Mmul_10/RefSeq Release 103 and calJac240_pri form a catarrhine-to-platyrrhine divergence ladder from one human source. Holding the source constant helps separate increasing divergence from source-annotation effects while each target release supplies destination-coordinate evidence.

The inference target is these seven transfers, not all eukaryotes or arbitrary cross-phylum mapping. A missing exact-release arm or repetition makes the planned cohort incomplete; successful cases cannot silently replace it.

### 4.2 Selecting assembly-matched target annotations

Target comparators are chosen by coordinate compatibility before recency. Using a newer annotation on another assembly would require another mapping algorithm and confound the evaluation. The acquisition lock binds NCBI assembly and annotation releases, artifacts, sizes, and hashes; for bee it additionally binds the MAKER/Figshare artifact, license, chromosome transform, and expected lengths.

Six targets use the best identified RefSeq release on the exact assembly, including GeneIDs for a provider-defined sensitivity analysis. Bee uses the annotation released with ASM1932182v1 because no suitable RefSeq release exists on that build. “Best” therefore means well annotated, released, traceable, and coordinate compatible—not merely newest or largest.

Acquisition is fail-closed. Downloaded artifacts are placed in a content-addressed object store. Archives are expanded deterministically, annotations are normalized only where the study manifest declares a transformation, sequence identifiers are checked against the target FASTA, and every derived view records its inputs. The annotation lock cannot be regenerated silently after execution. This protects against remote-file replacement and against the common error of evaluating a lift on one assembly with truth coordinates from another.

### 4.3 Uniform protein reciprocal-best-hit scope

Directly comparing every source gene with every target annotation row would mix orthologs, lineage-specific genes, paralogs, and incompatible isoform inventories. The primary target comparison instead constructs a uniform protein reciprocal-best-hit (RBH) scope for every transfer. Protein sequences are extracted from source and target annotations with the same phase- and strand-aware procedure. MMseqs2 searches in both directions with frozen identity, coverage, and E-value constraints. A source–target protein pair is retained only when each is the other's best supported hit under deterministic tie-breaking.

Transcript matches are then grouped to gene-level relationships through the annotation hierarchies. The preflight requires at least 100 gene groups and 100 transcript groups per transfer, preventing a superficially precise score from resting on a tiny matched set. RBH is applied uniformly even for same-species honey bee, so the primary score has a comparable construction across the cohort. The method emphasizes one-to-one, protein-supported content and deliberately leaves ambiguous families outside the primary denominator.

This scope improves comparability but creates matched-set bias. Genes with conserved proteins are more likely to enter than non-coding genes, rapidly evolving proteins, recent duplicates, or target-specific content. Consequently, the report presents two kinds of comprehensiveness: retention of the complete source hierarchy, which does not depend on RBH, and target-annotation recall/F1 within the frozen RBH scope. The first asks how much was carried forward; the second asks whether ortholog-supported structures agree. Neither alone is a complete biological assessment.

NCBI Gene's `gene_orthologs.gz` table supplies a separately labeled sensitivity analysis for cross-species targets where at least 100 unambiguous relationships map into both annotations. Only one-to-one relationships after source- and target-index filtering are retained. Same-taxon honey bee is marked not applicable. The provider-defined scope is never substituted for the protein-RBH primary result, and unavailable or low-coverage relationships remain explicit rather than being inferred.

### 4.4 Exact releases, canary, and paired execution

Both arms execute from clean, detached Git worktrees. The candidate SHA is the exact v1.0.11 tag; the reference SHA is the exact v1.0.8 tag. Before planning, the controller verifies `HEAD`, rejects tracked changes and unversioned import-affecting files, probes the imported `lifton` package from an isolated working directory, records supported CLI flags, and hashes all scientific inputs. `PYTHONPATH` is set separately for each arm, so the dirty development tree and post-tag modules cannot be imported accidentally.

The exact-v1.0.11 canary uses honey bee and human-to-gorilla because they span small and large annotations, insect and mammal biology, and same-species versus cross-species transfer. One run uses the fastest supported execution path and one uses the conservative path. Fast execution is admitted to the release comparison only if semantic feature multisets, score summaries, released-target comparisons, validators, and biological summaries agree for both canaries. Byte hashes are recorded to expose serialization differences but are not the semantic-equivalence criterion.

Each of the seven transfers then runs four paired repetitions with eight LiftOn threads. Odd repetitions execute v1.0.8 before v1.0.11; even repetitions reverse the order. This AB/BA balance reduces systematic effects from page cache, filesystem state, and changing shared-host load. The two arms of a repetition run sequentially so their wall and memory measurements do not directly contend with one another. At most two transfer cells are active concurrently, giving a planned maximum of 32 scheduler-accounted worker threads because each eight-thread CLI receives a conservative cost of 16.

The controller records admission policy, load and available-memory thresholds, command lines, environments, start and end times, exit status, watchdog state, manifests, output hashes, score artifacts, and process-group memory traces. A cell has a generous completion timeout and a separate no-progress timeout suitable for large mammalian alignments. It does not retry automatically. A scientific failure remains a failure until explicitly diagnosed; a successful candidate arm is not paired with a reference result from another repetition.

### 4.5 Metrics and denominators

**Source comprehensiveness.** Coding completeness is recovered source coding transcripts divided by the source coding denominator. All-feature completeness is identifier-based recovery across the counted source gene, transcript, ncRNA, pseudogene, and related hierarchy types; exon and CDS subfeatures are excluded from that aggregate. It does not use the published target annotation as its denominator. Stable-ID preservation is reported separately for exon and CDS feature types, including discontinuous CDS identifiers under a copy-aware namespace policy.

**Source-relative sequence and structure.** Recovered-set mean protein identity describes only transcripts for which a model exists. CovPI sums recovered protein identity over the complete source coding denominator, making omission costly. Recall at PI thresholds 0.50, 0.75, 0.90, and 0.95 shows whether a change is confined to weak models or reaches high-confidence proteins. Exact intron-chain recall compares strand-aware internal coding boundaries without requiring absolute source and target coordinates to match. ORF-valid recall requires a plausible start, terminal stop, and absence of internal stops. Common-set mean PI restricts both releases to transcripts recovered by both.

**Released-target coordinate concordance.** A predicted gene or transcript matches a scoped target feature when each interval covers at least the prespecified fraction of the other on the same sequence and strand. Primary reciprocal overlap is 0.50; 0.25 and 0.80 are sensitivity thresholds. Precision, recall, and F1 are reported for genes and transcripts. Exon, CDS, and intron-chain comparisons add structure beneath the matched transcript. These scores use target coordinates and therefore catch plausible source retention at the wrong or structurally incompatible locus.

**Released-target sequence concordance.** For the first repetition, phase-aware proteins are reconstructed from both predicted and released target CDS models. Exact-protein rate, mean protein identity, reciprocal length coverage, and ORF agreement provide coordinate-complementary evidence. Sequence scoring is limited to one repetition because deterministic outputs make repeated translation redundant; the other repetitions remain necessary for resource and determinism estimates.

**Validity and determinism.** Every arm is checked for parseability, identifier uniqueness, parent/child coherence, ordering, target sequence membership, coordinate bounds, and required local relationships. Candidate determinism requires output bytes, semantic content, source-score summaries, target-coordinate summaries, and first-repetition sequence evidence to agree wherever comparable. A valid result can still be biologically wrong, and a deterministic result can repeat the same mistake; both gates are necessary but not sufficient.

**Performance.** Elapsed time covers the complete annotation command, including DNA and protein evidence generation, locus processing, validation, and publication. The primary memory measure is the peak one-second sampled sum of RSS over the command's process group. Candidate/reference ratios are calculated within paired repetitions and summarized within transfer by the median. Values below one favor v1.0.11.

### 4.6 Statistical protocol and qualification gates

The transfer, not an individual repetition, is the unit of biological generalization. Repetitions measure order and execution variation within a transfer. Aggregate candidate-minus-reference quality differences and candidate/reference resource ratios use an equal-transfer cluster bootstrap: transfers are resampled with replacement, and their four within-transfer paired observations move together. Ten thousand replicates and seed `20260813` are fixed in the study manifest. Confidence intervals are percentile intervals and reflect heterogeneity among the seven chosen transfers, not uncertainty across arbitrary species or computers.

The cohort gate requires all seven transfers and all 28 pairs. Every candidate repetition must be valid; each transfer must be deterministic. The lower 95% confidence bound for every aggregate source-quality delta must be at least −0.001, and no transfer's worst source-quality delta may fall below −0.01. Candidate gene and transcript locus F1 against the released annotation must each be at least 0.50, and no transfer's worst target-concordance delta may be below −0.01.

For performance, the upper 95% confidence bound of both the wall-time and process-group-RSS geometric-mean ratio must not exceed 1.10. Each transfer's median wall and memory ratio must not exceed 1.25. A conservative two-concurrent-transfer candidate memory proxy—the sum of the two largest observed candidate process-group peaks—must not exceed 192 GiB. These gates deliberately combine an aggregate requirement with an outlier cap. A very fast majority cannot hide one operationally severe regression.

### 5.1 Reading recovery from a biological perspective

Comprehensiveness is evaluated before asking whether a recovered model is accurate. The distinction matters most in asymmetric annotations: a richly annotated human or mouse source can contain more isoforms and non-coding hierarchy than the target release, while a same-species assembly transfer should retain nearly all source content unless assembly or representation differences intervene. Coding completeness tests whether protein-coding transcript models survive. All-feature completeness tests whether LiftOn carries the broader annotation graph, including gene-like parents and non-coding features that a protein-only method cannot reconstruct by itself.

The two measures move in opposite directions here, and that divergence is the central comprehensiveness result. Across the seven transfers the equal-transfer paired delta in all-feature completeness is **+0.0180 (95% CI +0.0088 to +0.0269)**, while the coding-completeness delta is **−0.0035 (95% CI −0.0055 to −0.0016)**. Both prespecified bootstrap intervals exclude zero, although the 50-gate design is not a multiplicity-adjusted hypothesis suite. v1.0.11 carries more of the counted hierarchy and slightly fewer coding transcript models; §5.3 characterizes those models.

### 5.2 Coding recovery and hierarchy breadth

Coding recovery is essentially unchanged where the source and target are closely related and declines slightly as divergence increases. On the honey-bee assembly transfer v1.0.11 recovers marginally more (0.99548 against 0.99544); on the fruit-fly transfer marginally fewer (0.97357 against 0.97403). The five mammalian transfers show consistent, small reductions: −0.0040 for human→gorilla, −0.0036 for mouse→*M. caroli*, −0.0024 for dog→cat, −0.0070 for human→macaque, and −0.0073 for human→marmoset. No individual transfer approaches the −0.01 per-transfer floor, and every per-transfer source-quality gate passes.

All-feature completeness moves the other way and by a larger margin. The gain is negligible where the counted source hierarchy is dominated by coding content (honey bee +0.0008, fruit fly +0.0020) and substantial for the richer mammalian annotations: +0.0289 for human→gorilla, +0.0367 for mouse→*M. caroli*, +0.0151 for dog→cat, +0.0228 for human→macaque, and +0.0196 for human→marmoset. This pattern is consistent with the cumulative endpoint's broader handling of gene-like hierarchies, but the comparison is not an ablation and cannot assign the gain to one mechanism.

The absolute all-feature values also show why these measures must not be pooled. Human-sourced transfers recover under 0.41 of counted source features against under 0.38 for v1.0.8. Those levels use the complete counted source hierarchy, whereas coding completeness uses coding transcripts and target concordance uses frozen ortholog groups. Each denominator answers a different question; the paired all-feature difference is more interpretable than comparing its absolute level with another metric family.

### 5.4 Released-target recall and stable identifiers

Target gene recall asks how many RBH-scoped released genes have a lifted locus meeting the reciprocal-overlap rule. Target transcript recall is stricter because alternative isoforms and exon structures must be represented in a compatible interval. Both are close between the releases, and both track the source-side pattern: the small coding-recovery difference on the mammalian transfers appears as a correspondingly small transcript-recall difference, and no transfer breaches the per-transfer target-delta floor.

CDS and exon identifier preservation is the largest single difference measured anywhere in this study. Downstream databases and comparative pipelines routinely join annotations by stable IDs, and preserving a feature's coordinates while changing its identifier silently breaks those joins. v1.0.11 preserves **0.866 to 0.995** of CDS identifiers and 0.856 to 0.990 of exon identifiers across the seven transfers. v1.0.8 preserves **0.052 to 0.700** and 0.289 to 0.708 respectively, with the worst case on the fruit-fly transfer, where only 5.2 % of CDS identifiers survive. The measure is copy-aware and type-aware, so a discontinuous CDS does not create an artificial penalty.

This large endpoint difference must not be attributed to the narrow v1.0.11 patch. All seven study annotations ingest under both releases; the patch resolves a collision between a generated duplicate-name suffix and an existing identifier. The preservation result instead measures cumulative identifier-allocation behavior between v1.0.8 and v1.0.11. It is an operational continuity property, not evidence of locus correctness, so validity and target concordance remain separate.

## 6. Accuracy and structural-quality results

### 6.1 Two complementary definitions of agreement

The source-relative view asks whether LiftOn retained the annotated protein and internal coding organization it was asked to transfer. The target-relative view asks whether that result agrees with a released annotation generated on the destination assembly. These views can disagree for legitimate reasons. A source transcript may represent an isoform not present in the target release, a genuine target-specific change may reduce source identity, or the released target annotation may omit a conserved structure. Conversely, excellent source protein retention at a paralogous locus can look strong until coordinate concordance is examined.

The primary target comparison is deliberately ortholog-scoped and reports both precision and recall through F1. It does not compare total gene counts as though every source and target gene had a one-to-one biological relation. Target protein identity is reconstructed from the exact target sequence, so it tests whether corresponding coding models imply similar proteins even when exon boundaries differ. Intron-chain and CDS structure add stricter constraints that protein identity alone can miss.

### 6.2 Protein retention, threshold recall, and common sets

CovPI is the principal source-protein metric because it couples identity to recovery: a candidate that drops difficult transcripts cannot improve it merely by reporting an excellent mean among survivors. Its equal-transfer paired delta is **−0.0015 (95% CI −0.0035 to +0.0006)**, an interval that contains zero. Threshold recall behaves the same way at every cut examined: −0.0010 at identity 0.50, −0.0006 at 0.75, −0.0006 at 0.90 and −0.0005 at 0.95, each with a confidence interval spanning zero.

The interpretation is that coupling identity to recovery very nearly cancels the two effects described in §5. v1.0.11 recovers slightly fewer coding models, and the models it does recover score slightly higher, with the result that no aggregate protein-retention measure is distinguishable from parity. This is a more informative outcome than a nominal win would have been: it locates the difference between these releases somewhere other than in per-transcript protein retention.

### 6.3 Intron chains and open reading frames

Exact intron-chain recall is a stringent structural measure. It ignores absolute genomic position but requires the ordered internal CDS-boundary pattern to match the source model, so a protein can remain nearly identical after a shifted splice boundary and still fail it. Its paired delta is **−0.0013 (95% CI −0.0027 to −0.00007)**, an interval that just excludes zero. ORF-valid recall, evaluated over the complete source denominator, is **+0.0001 (95% CI −0.0017 to +0.0023)** and is indistinguishable from parity.

Per transfer, structural recall improves on the fruit fly and dog→cat transfers and declines slightly on the human-sourced primate transfers. The aggregate combines differential recovery with small, mixed changes among transcripts recovered by both releases. Because no decomposition was prespecified, the data do not support assigning the delta exclusively to either source.

### 6.4 Concordance with the released target annotations

The target-relative sequence view moves consistently. Within each arm's frozen ortholog-linked comparison, v1.0.11 has a higher exact translated-protein agreement rate, mean target protein identity, and predicted-ORF-valid rate on all seven transfers. Exact agreement rises by 0.00019 to 0.00205, mean identity by 0.00014 to 0.00325, and ORF validity by 0.00054 to 0.00651. Coverage-weighted identity and reciprocal length coverage move the same way. These measures are complementary but correlated; ORF validity adds structural information, whereas the identity-derived measures should not be counted as independent confirmations. The result is a consistent endpoint pattern, not a mechanistic attribution.

Locus placement, by contrast, is a tie. The equal-transfer paired delta in target gene-locus F1 is **−0.0005 (95% CI −0.0014 to +0.0002)** and in transcript-locus F1 **−0.0004 (95% CI −0.0012 to +0.0001)**; both intervals contain zero, and no per-transfer gene F1 differs by more than 0.003. Structural agreement within matched transcript groups — intron chain, exon and CDS F1 — is likewise indistinguishable between the releases.

Taken together with §5 and §6.2, this supports a narrower statement: **gene-locus F1 has no statistically distinguishable release difference, while v1.0.11 has modestly higher target-sequence concordance within each arm's frozen ortholog-linked comparison.**

The absolute transcript-locus statistics require one denominator caveat. The published honey-bee comparator contains exactly 12,288 genes and 12,288 mRNAs, whereas LiftOn transfers source isoforms. Within the fixed 8,556-gene/8,556-transcript RBH preflight scope, extra predicted isoforms lower precision even when the comparator simply does not represent them.

The honey-bee transcript-locus recall is 0.810 and gene-locus F1 is 0.777, but transcript-locus F1 is 0.460 because precision is low under that asymmetric isoform inventory. This is a plausible interpretation of the comparator behavior, not a causal correction. The prespecified score and failed floor remain unchanged.

### 6.6 What the accuracy results can and cannot establish

This evaluation is stronger than checking source protein identity alone because it uses a destination-coordinate annotation selected independently of LiftOn output. It can detect wrong-locus placement, broad interval mismatch, missing target orthologs, incompatible splice structures, coding differences, and invalid ORFs. It also provides precision as well as recall, so indiscriminate extra predictions are not rewarded.

It cannot establish expression, tissue specificity, functional equivalence, or the uniquely correct isoform. Both annotations can inherit shared public evidence; target releases are themselves computational products; and RBH favors conserved one-to-one proteins. A target-specific loss can make perfect source retention wrong, while an unannotated but real target isoform can make LiftOn appear falsely discordant. Claims in this report are therefore phrased as source retention, target concordance, and structural plausibility. "Biologically validated" would require independent curated loci, transcriptomic/proteomic evidence not used by either annotation, or synthetic mappings with known truth.

## 7. Runtime and memory results

### 7.1 Complete-command elapsed time

Elapsed time begins before external evidence generation and ends after validation and atomic publication. It therefore answers the operational question a user faces when invoking LiftOn on two assemblies, not merely the cost of the Python locus stage. Each transfer's primary statistic is the median candidate/reference ratio across four AB/BA repetitions.

v1.0.11 is faster on every transfer. Per-transfer median ratios run from **0.547** on the honey-bee transfer to **0.852** on dog→cat, with the equal-transfer geometric mean at **0.703 (95% CI 0.632 to 0.773)**. The confidence interval lies entirely below parity, and the worst individual transfer is far inside the 1.25 per-transfer cap. Weighting by observed duration rather than giving each transfer one vote gives a total-work ratio of **0.727**: 13.31 hours of candidate wall time against 18.30 hours for the reference across the cohort.

In absolute terms the saving is largest where runs are longest. The human-sourced primate transfers fall from 4.03, 4.09 and 4.39 hours to 2.84, 3.24 and 3.14 hours; mouse→*M. caroli* falls from 3.01 to 1.90 hours. The insect transfers, at well under an hour either way, contribute the most favourable ratios but the least operational benefit — which is precisely why the equal-transfer mean and the duration-weighted ratio are both reported.

### 7.2 Process-group memory

Command-level `ru_maxrss` observes only the profiled process and can miss memory held by aligner children, so this study also samples the whole process group once per second and sums resident memory across observed processes. The process-group figure is the primary memory statistic; the command-level maximum is supplemental, and the two differ substantially — on the honey-bee transfer the candidate's command-level peak is 3.8 GiB while its process-group peak is 22.0 GiB.

On the process-group measure v1.0.11 uses less memory on every transfer, by a consistent but modest margin. Per-transfer median ratios span **0.871 to 0.932**, and the equal-transfer geometric mean is **0.895 (95% CI 0.883 to 0.909)**. Absolute medians rise steeply with genome and annotation size: 22.0 GiB for the honey bee, 24.7 GiB for the fruit fly, 81.8 GiB for dog→cat, 110.6 GiB for mouse→*M. caroli*, and 157 to 176 GiB for the three human-sourced primate transfers, against 24.7 to 188.3 GiB for the reference.

Summed RSS is deliberately labelled a proxy. Shared pages may be counted in more than one process, the sampler can miss sub-second spikes, and overlapping external programs can shift the peak; these limitations apply to both arms but need not cancel exactly, because process topology differs. The two-concurrent-transfer figure adds the two largest candidate peaks, matching the scheduler's maximum concurrent cells, and reaches **342.4 GiB**. It is conservative for admission planning and is not a synchronised measurement that those two peaks coincided — but it is large enough to matter operationally, and §8.4 records that it exceeds the ceiling this study declared in advance.

### 7.3 Configuration asymmetry and causal interpretation

One design decision conditions every number in this section and is stated here rather than deferred to the limitations. Each release was run in the fastest configuration its own command-line interface supports, determined by capability negotiation rather than assumed. v1.0.11 therefore ran with streaming input, in-memory Liftoff hand-off, locus-level parallelism and the native binding enabled; v1.0.8 ran with only the thread count and copy handling it accepts. The comparison is between two releases as a user would actually invoke them, not between two builds of the same engine under identical flags, and the performance results should be read as an endpoint comparison in that sense.

Within that framing the difference cannot be attributed to any single mechanism. The endpoint spans concurrent evidence generation, bounded protein alignment, locus parallelism, cache behaviour, hierarchy handling, rescue and validation, and the dominant phase varies with genome size, annotation complexity, divergence and the number of loci entering rescue. A faster result supports the practical claim about the release endpoint; it does not establish that any one optimisation is responsible.

The host was shared rather than dedicated. AB/BA ordering, pairwise ratios, admission thresholds and four repetitions reduce but do not eliminate noise from filesystem traffic, competing processes, NUMA placement and page cache. Two of the twenty-eight repetitions were re-executed after a scheduling defect in the benchmark harness admitted more concurrent whole-genome cells than its own limit allowed, and those two were measured at lower concurrency than their siblings; their outputs are byte-identical to the other three repetitions of the same transfers, so the biological results are unaffected, but their absolute timings were taken under lighter load. Confidence intervals quantify heterogeneity across the chosen transfers, not reproducibility on other hardware.

## 8. Validity, determinism, and qualification verdict

### 8.1 Completion is a scientific gate

The planned cohort completed: 28 of 28 paired results and four repetitions for every transfer. The reducer requires all exact-release pair documents and never averages survivors after a failure, which would bias biological and performance summaries.

Six cells initially failed, all through the v1.0.8 arm. Four dog→cat lifts completed but could not be scored because one reference mRNA had inverted coordinates; two primate repetitions hit allocation errors after scheduler over-admission. The first group was re-scored after the evaluator could record and quarantine the row, and the latter two were re-executed at reduced concurrency. Section 11 audits both repairs.

### 8.2 Validity and determinism

**All 28 candidate outputs are valid, with zero errors**, under complete semantic, streaming structural, and target-FASTA bounds checks. Every reference transfer records validation errors: 76,070 per bee output; 572,461 for fruit fly; 1,239,592 for gorilla; 914,253 for *M. caroli*; 619,612 for cat; 1,324,361 for macaque; and 1,331,997 for marmoset. Most are duplicate identifiers, the defect class reflected by §5.4's preservation measure.

Determinism holds for all seven transfers: each release's four outputs are byte-identical within a transfer and derived summaries agree. The two later, lower-concurrency re-executions also reproduce their siblings bit for bit.

Neither property establishes correctness: a deterministic locus can be wrong and a valid graph can select a paralog. They instead show that downstream tools can parse the measured files and that release differences are not scheduling variation.

### 8.3 Structural integrity of the compared annotations

The evaluator records and skips structurally unusable target rows rather than silently losing an annotation. **v1.0.11 produced none.** v1.0.8 produced one in every dog→cat repetition: an mRNA at record 333,910 spanning 62,472,244 to 62,461,173, with start greater than end. Its exclusion is explicit in the evidence.

One unparseable feature among 1,808,290 GFF3 records can stop a strict reader; here it initially prevented four completed pairs from being scored. Quarantine preserves the remaining evidence while recording the exclusion.

## 9. Discussion

### 9.1 What the results support

The failed conjunctive contract supports a narrow endpoint claim while refusing a broader release qualification.

What is supported is an endpoint difference with several independent operational dimensions. Every candidate output passes structural validation where every v1.0.8 output records errors; identifier preservation improves from as little as 0.052 to 0.866 or better; every transfer is faster and uses less sampled process-group memory; and the target-sequence metrics are modestly higher on all seven transfers. Gene-locus F1 is statistically tied. Determinism holds across repetitions and scheduling conditions.

v1.0.11 does not recover more coding content: it recovers slightly less while carrying more of the broader source hierarchy, and the missing models are enriched for weak protein matches. Whether that exchange is acceptable depends on whether a consumer prioritizes conservative coding models or maximal candidate recovery.

The separately released target annotations add evidence unavailable from source retention alone: they ask whether retained content overlaps another annotation project on the destination coordinates and implies similar proteins. They remain computational comparators, can share upstream evidence with RefSeq sources, and do not establish independent biological truth. Their value is complementary measurement, not conversion of concordance into validation.

### 9.2 Tradeoffs that should remain visible

Recovery and concordance pull in different directions. The v1.0.11 endpoint has slightly lower coding recovery, higher all-feature recovery, valid and more identifier-stable files, and modestly higher target-sequence concordance. The experiment was not factorial, so these changes cannot be causally assigned to conservative selection, hierarchy handling, rescue, or another individual mechanism. Keeping the measurements separate prevents one favorable endpoint from concealing another unfavorable one.

Performance and biology are gated independently so speed cannot purchase a pass by dropping difficult content. The endpoint is faster and uses less memory per transfer, yet the measured large-transfer peaks refute the planned two-transfer memory ceiling. The design cannot isolate the contributions of concurrency, bounded alignment, rescue, or validation.

Dataset-level tables are therefore essential. Aggregate estimates describe the typical selected transfer; rows expose lineage, annotation-policy, and assembly differences. All per-transfer non-regression deltas pass, while eight aggregate lower bounds and the honey-bee absolute transcript floor do not.

### 9.3 Implications for annotation practice

For users, the largest practical differences are parseable output, stable identifiers, deterministic files, roughly seven-tenths the wall time, and nine-tenths the sampled process-group memory. These properties determine whether a lift-over integrates safely into a pipeline.

The result also argues for scoring lift-overs against a separately released annotation on the destination assembly wherever one exists, with explicit assembly, provider, scope, and isoform-policy caveats. Source-relative identity alone reports near parity; the target comparison, validation, and identifier measures reveal other endpoint differences.

The memory result is also a capacity warning: human-sourced primate transfers peak near 170 GiB of summed RSS, so two should not be admitted under a 192-GiB ceiling.

### 9.4 Release interpretation

v1.0.11 is a single-fix release on top of v1.0.10, and the measured endpoint difference against v1.0.8 is cumulative rather than attributable to that fix. The patch itself addresses an ingestion failure in which a generated duplicate-identifier name collided with a name already present, aborting the run; that failure mode does not appear in this cohort because every input here ingests under both releases.

The cohort verdict is FAIL and the release-level label remains diagnostic. This is not an across-the-board regression finding: the coding-completeness tradeoff is small but real, the intron-chain interval also excludes zero without a prespecified decomposition, six other source bounds fail on their lower limits, the bee floor is conditioned by comparator asymmetry, and the memory ceiling is genuinely exceeded. Conversely, the endpoint is better on validity, identifier stability, target-sequence concordance, elapsed time, and sampled process-group memory across the selected transfers.

## 10. Limitations

1. **Inference boundary.** Seven selected transfers cover one same-species insect assembly comparison, one insect species comparison, and five mammalian comparisons. They do not establish behavior for plants, fungi, polyploids, fragmented assemblies, very distant species, or every supported input dialect.

2. **Comparison annotations are not truth.** RefSeq and the published bee annotation provide exact target coordinates but remain computational releases that can omit isoforms, contain errors, or share evidence with source annotations. No independent RNA-seq, long-read, proteomic, functional, or curated-locus evidence was used.

3. **Matched-set and annotation-policy bias.** Protein RBH enriches for conserved one-to-one coding genes and underrepresents duplicates, non-coding genes, rapidly evolving genes, and lineage-specific content. Provider relationships are incomplete. Differences in curation and isoform multiplicity, especially the one-transcript-per-gene bee comparator, affect precision and recall.

4. **Endpoint rather than ablation.** The releases differ in several algorithmic and execution capabilities. The study measures their deployable endpoints and cannot assign recovery, concordance, identifier, time, or memory changes to a single mechanism.

5. **Shared-host performance.** AB/BA order and paired ratios reduce systematic bias but cannot eliminate cache state, NUMA effects, filesystem contention, or unrelated load. One-second summed process-group RSS can double-count shared pages or miss brief peaks; the concurrency proxy adds observed maxima rather than measuring simultaneous physical use.

6. **Repetition and statistical scope.** Four repetitions expose order and gross variability, not long-term hardware variation. Bootstrap intervals resample seven transfers as clusters and do not describe a universal genome population. The 50 conjunctive engineering gates are qualification rules, not a multiple-testing-adjusted hypothesis suite; their confidence limits are estimation and decision bounds.

7. **Threshold and scoring scope.** Coordinate results depend on the 0.50 reciprocal-overlap rule, with 0.25 and 0.80 used only for sensitivity. Target-sequence reconstruction is performed once per arm after output determinism is established. Structural validation is necessary but cannot prove orthology, expression, function, or the uniquely correct isoform.

8. **Audited repairs.** The original execution produced paired artifacts for 26 of 28 planned cells, but only 22 were initially scoreable. Four dog→cat artifacts were scored after a single inverted-coordinate v1.0.8 row could be quarantined; one macaque and one marmoset repetition were re-executed after scheduler over-admission caused v1.0.8 allocation failures. Re-scoring preserved metrics, and re-executed outputs are byte-identical to their sibling repetitions, although their resource samples reflect lighter host load.

9. **Prespecified bee floor.** The absolute transcript-locus floor assumes comparable isoform inventories. That assumption is poor for the bee comparator, but the observed failure is retained without post-hoc substitution.

10. **Historical and future evidence.** The appendix uses functional SHA `7fc8419` under a different protocol and cannot alter the exact-tag verdict. No sealed `canonical-v2` truth-aware result exists; curated loci, synthetic mappings, scaling, and additional dialects remain future work.

## 11. Reproducibility and evidence audit

### 11.1 Ordinary use

The positional FASTA order for an ordinary LiftOn invocation is target first, reference second:

```console
lifton -g reference.gff3 -o lifton.gff3 target.fa reference.fa
```

That order is intentional. The reference GFF3 describes `reference.fa`; annotations are generated on `target.fa`. Real runs also require minimap2 and miniprot on `PATH`.

### 11.2 Frozen study preparation

Study preparation uses a declarative manifest that records the two exact SHAs, seven biological pairs, execution policy, statistics, gates, target annotation releases, and the honey-bee sequence-name mapping. Acquisition and preparation are separate so network state cannot change after a campaign begins. Representative commands are:


Long jobs run in detached tmux sessions, while controller state, logs, artifacts, heartbeats, and immutable plans—not session presence—define progress and completion.

### 11.3 Canary and exact-release campaign

The paired controller first compares fast and conservative exact-v1.0.11 execution on bee and human→gorilla and writes fingerprinted `fast-safe-canary.json`. Profile `biology-v1` then binds the seven IDs, four repetitions, eight threads, capability negotiation, released-target scoring, and balanced order.

Before execution, the plan binds both worktree SHAs, executable, datasets, preflight, resources, timeouts, and admission policy. Each arm imports from its own clean worktree; SHA, input, capability, or cell-directory drift is fatal.

### 11.4 Evidence repairs and how they were audited

Two repairs were needed before the cohort was complete, and both are auditable rather than asserted.

The **structural-record quarantine** records and skips an unusable target-annotation row instead of discarding the annotation. Because it changed scoring after 22 cells, `score-pair` rebuilt every result from its arm's release manifest, rehashed the published GFF3, and called the original scoring function. All 22 reproduced exactly except for the new integrity block, its artifact hash, and evaluator runtime; no metric, validation result, fingerprint, or lift profile changed. The four previously unscored dog→cat cells then used the same path.

The **scheduler admission fix** made launched-but-not-yet-listable workers count against capacity. Two lost repetitions were re-executed in a continuation bound to the parent plan and host; both arms are byte-identical to their three sibling repetitions.

For provider sensitivity only, NCBI Gene relationships outside the scored source scope are counted and dropped. The primary RBH scope remains fail-closed on unknown identifiers.

The historical pattern illustrates why protein identity and recovery must be reported together. Liftoff preserves reference organization well when nucleotide synteny remains informative, but recovery falls at very large evolutionary distances. Miniprot often retains far more coding evidence there, yet it is not a hierarchy-preserving annotation lift and can select paralogous protein matches. The measured LiftOn candidate generally improves recovered-set protein identity over Liftoff while preserving a source-structured annotation, but at the most distant transfers its recovery remains far below unconstrained miniprot evidence. That is an accuracy–recovery tradeoff, not evidence that one tool dominates every evolutionary regime.

Some historical v1.0.8 runs produced partial output or crashed, and later functional code recovered substantially more source coding content. Those observations are useful robustness history but cannot be converted into paired speed advantages: a partial or failed run has not completed the same work. Historical validator counts also use older reporting limits and are not presented as a commensurate leaderboard.

The appendix supports two forward-looking conclusions. First, complete genome transfer should be evaluated on biologically defensible pairs with target-coordinate evidence wherever possible, which motivates the present redesign. Second, very distant mapping needs a different truth-aware design that can distinguish lost synteny, correct protein-only rescue, paralogy, and true lineage-specific gain or loss. Such experiments belong to future `canonical-v2` work. No sealed `canonical-v2` measurements exist, so this report does not preview their result.

## 13. Conclusion

This study replaced a mixed execution inventory with one prespecified biological question: how exact LiftOn v1.0.11 and exact v1.0.8 behave when each transfers a complete source annotation to seven complete target genomes, and the result is compared with a released annotation on the same target assembly. The design bound raw inputs, annotations, ortholog scopes, exact code, paired order, resources, statistics and report generation, and it reported recovery, source-relative sequence and structure, target-coordinate and target-sequence concordance, identifier continuity, validity, determinism, time and memory without collapsing them into one score.

All seven transfers completed in four paired repetitions each, and the cohort **did not pass** its conjunctive contract: forty of fifty prespecified gates passed and ten failed. The memory ceiling is genuinely exceeded; the honey-bee floor is sensitive to a one-transcript-per-gene comparator; coding completeness is slightly lower; intron-chain recall is also lower without a prespecified causal decomposition; and six further source bounds fail on their lower confidence limits. No gate was redefined after its result was seen.

The same evidence supports a narrower endpoint claim. Every one of the twenty-eight v1.0.11 outputs passes structural validation where every v1.0.8 output records errors; CDS identifier preservation rises from as little as 0.052 to at least 0.866; every selected transfer is faster and uses less sampled process-group memory, with equal-transfer geometric-mean ratios of 0.703× and 0.895×; and target-sequence concordance is modestly higher on all seven transfers within each arm's frozen comparison. The gene-locus F1 difference is not statistically distinguishable from zero. These results support file validity, identifier continuity, computational efficiency, and sequence/structure plausibility—not categorical identity of loci or independently proven biological correctness.

The verdict exposes rather than averages away a genuine tradeoff: stronger file integrity and modestly higher target-sequence concordance accompany slightly lower coding recovery. Its acceptability depends on whether a consumer prioritizes conservative, joinable models or maximal candidate recovery.

Finally, comparison with a separately released target annotation strengthens, but does not transform, the biological claim. It tests agreement with another representation on the destination coordinates; it does not prove expression, functional orthology, or unique locus truth. The report therefore ends with a diagnostic verdict and an explicit evidence boundary rather than a release-wide PASS.
